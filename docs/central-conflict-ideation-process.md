# Central-conflict ideation process

This document records the correction to the prime-mover ideation process. It is
a working design method, not setting canon. The moderator's historical novelty
record lives in [the motif ledger](../.panel/prime-mover-motif-ledger.md).

## Diagnosis

The project currently has **too much prompt structure and too little world
structure**.

The established constraints are good at defining tone, exclusions, and the
place of necromancy. They are not yet enough to locate a central conflict inside
one shared present-day Maldirin. An ideator is still being asked to invent, in a
single response:

- the relevant cosmological rule;
- the present political and geographic order;
- the historical pressure already acting on that order;
- the mover, objective, means, and reach;
- the player's reason to care;
- the recurring campaign loop;
- the ending space.

When several models perform that much upstream invention from the same long
negative brief, they fall back on reusable fantasy structures: doors, roads,
bells, dead gods, restored territories, displaced claimants, hidden keystones,
and morally ambivalent redistribution. Adding every failure as another explicit
prohibition makes the prompt longer without giving the next candidate more
positive ground to stand on.

The earlier magic brief created another attractor. By treating environmental
conditions and providers as the dominant grammar of supernatural capability, it
encouraged conflicts about severing, translating, monopolizing, relocating, or
invalidating local powers. Future ideation must use the corrected
source/method/consequence model. A place-dependent conflict remains valid, but
it receives no automatic preference for resembling the old system.

The correction is asymmetric:

- **More world structure:** decide a small number of load-bearing facts about
  present Maldirin.
- **Less ideator-prompt structure:** give each generator a short positive brief,
  a decision target, and a few genuine non-negotiables.
- **More process structure:** preserve independent divergence, deduplicate with a
  moderator-only ledger, use the user's interest ratings as the primary taste
  signal, and develop only survivors.

One further distinction is mandatory: intelligible motivation does not mean
ordinary psychology enlarged to supernatural scale. A central mover may be
grieving, ashamed, jealous, or traumatized, but the campaign cannot reduce to an
exceptional being forcing Maldirin to resolve its private wound. Its objective
must retain world-directed substance when that formative wound is removed.

## Minimum viable world before another broad wave

This is not a demand for an atlas. Each anchor can be answered provisionally in
two to four sentences. Together they give independent ideators the same world
without determining the story for them.

The current working answers are recorded in
[the present-day campaign frame](world.md#provisional-present-day-campaign-frame).

### 1. Campaign horizon

Establish the physical and social scale across which the first long campaign can
operate: one large landmass, several connected regions, a frontier between
orders, or another legible horizon. Decide roughly how travel, news, armies, and
supernatural influence move across it.

### 2. Present macro-order

Establish three to five broad human or nonhuman powers, zones, or forms of
authority that presently matter. These are not detailed factions. Their purpose
is to tell us what can be threatened, conquered, divided, preserved, reformed,
or allied.

### 3. Independent historical pressure

Choose at least one major change already underway before the prime mover acts:
conquest, recovery after collapse, migration, succession crisis, ecological
change, failing infrastructure, renewed trade, doctrinal fracture, or something
less familiar. This keeps the world from becoming a stage that exists only for
the villain.

### 4. Situated stakes

Establish how aspirations arise from local histories, attachments, dependencies,
injuries, and opportunities. Do not invent a universal aspiration. Include
positive projects that can be endangered without reducing all stakes to survival
or wealth; treat leaving something worth inheriting as one strong recurring
driver rather than Maldirin's common purpose.

### 5. Supernatural power ceiling

Clarify what exceptional powers can usually do without a once-in-history breach,
what makes the prime mover exceptional, and what kinds of direct agents or
manifestations can recur in play. Keep the precise first cause and full taxonomy
open. Do not make a God depend on a local aspect, adherent, site, or worshipper
merely to keep the campaign manageable. Such interfaces can provide precision,
persistence, legibility, or vulnerability while the greater agency remains
capable of acting. Divine limits should be proportionate to divine scale.

### 6. Dramatic interface

Choose a preference, not a law, for how the campaign meets its mover: a recurring
embodied antagonist; an inaccessible power with durable agents; a mobile force;
an environmental or cosmic process with intentional interventions; or a mixed
form. This prevents every pitch from hiding its cause until the late game.

### 7. First-campaign promise

Define the opportunities and agency an ordinary opening chapter should provide,
not a mandatory sequence or ten-hour checklist. The player need not learn the
whole cosmology or pursue the main conflict. Refusal, delay, travel, failure,
different play styles, and successor circumstances may change when or whether
particular beats occur; world actors advance their aims for grounded reasons,
not because a plot clock is punishing disengagement.

These anchors should remain broad enough that Leashmaker, an external power
raising a city's dead, Army Behind Sleep, an ecological invasion, a mortal using
an exceptional supernatural instrument, and a cosmic agency can all still be
valid answers.

## Lean ideator brief

After the anchors are set, a generator should receive:

1. a one-page positive account of present Maldirin;
2. the decision: propose a campaign-scale prime mover and conflict that can
   sustain multiple testaments;
3. no more than six hard constraints that protect actual product requirements;
4. one assigned exploration lens that changes the source of ideas rather than
   prescribing the answer;
5. a compact output schema.

The mover's identity, desire, supernatural nature, and capability should be
conceived as one causal whole. Do not require an “objective without magic”; that
overcorrection favors human-scale compulsions with an unrelated magical trick
attached afterward. Require instead that even an alien or cosmological objective
be explainable in plain causal language.

The brief should state that magic may be bodily, learned, material, artifact-
borne, divine, relational, collective, temporal, environmental, or stranger. Do
not ask every candidate to explain which local condition its mover controls.

The first pass should use no proper nouns and little atmospheric prose. It must
not prove multiple strategies, succession behavior, or a constructive ending;
those convergent demands caused raw generation to favor reformable
infrastructure and allocation disputes. Each candidate is only a causal kernel:

```text
ID
Mover class and present objective
What the mover deliberately does now
Why that action reaches ordinary localities
Immediate human danger
Exceptional capability and specific exposure
Recurring dramatic contact
First playable intervention
What makes the pressure larger than one incident
Causal signature in one sentence
```

For a divine mover, `specific exposure` means how the campaign can affect this
intervention—an aspect, army, manifestation, commitment, rival, contradiction,
or costly consequence. It does not require a hidden local battery whose removal
turns off the God.

Suitable exploration lenses include different dramatic sources—personal
enmity, military pressure, natural or ecological agency, religious encounter,
precursor intervention, extra-Maldirian contact, embodied monstrosity, or a
strange testament loop. They should not be pseudo-random baskets of required
nouns or lists of banned motifs.

## Wide-to-narrow workflow

### Phase 1: independent divergence

- Agents work independently and do not see one another's submissions, prior
  rankings, or the motif ledger.
- Generate causal kernels, not dossiers.
- Separate invention from constraint repair. Do not require raw kernels to solve
  strategy breadth, persistence, or ending space. A kernel that later cannot
  satisfy a hard rule cleanly is rejected rather than blurred together with
  another idea.

### Phase 2: moderator novelty and coherence screen

- Reduce each kernel to its causal signature.
- Compare it against the ledger and the current batch.
- Reject prose that withholds a necessary causal link or uses an evocative image
  as a substitute for meaning.
- Reject a central objective that disappears when the mover's formative trauma
  or unmet emotional need is removed. Personality may change how the mover acts;
  it may not be the campaign's only cause.
- Remove names and model provenance from the presentation.
- Do not rank survivors before the user has seen them.

### Phase 3: user spark screen

Present no more than eight to twelve plain-language kernels. Ask only whether
each is exciting, worth repairing, merely interesting, or dead. Record the
actual distribution; do not reinterpret “intriguing” as “finalist.”

### Phase 4: selective development

Develop two to four survivors independently. Each dossier must prove:

- a coherent opening chapter whose pace and sequence can vary by play style;
- recurring encounters with the mover, agents, or unmistakable consequences;
- regional stories that retain independent provenance;
- multiple strategies without mandatory economy, conquest, or politics;
- compatible opportunities for every necromantic practice without making
  necromancy the master key;
- a broad ending space, including at least one costly constructive outcome;
- persistence across failure, death, succession, and world advancement.

A constructive possibility is evidence that the conflict need not end only in
annihilation or surrender. It is not a requirement to domesticate the mover into
an office, treaty, federation, or regulatory service. Dossiers must also support
playable continuation states after partial victory, defeat, refusal, exploitation,
or unresolved conflict. Scene prototypes should remain compelling if no
continent-wide settlement is ever reached.

### Phase 5: adversarial review and repair

Use at least two independent critics with different evaluation emphases. Critics
see the same anonymous dossiers and rubric, not model family or prior judge
preferences. Their job is to identify fatal weaknesses and exact repair
boundaries, not invent a consensus hybrid. Repair a candidate only if its causal
identity survives.

## Evaluation plan

Treat prompt changes as experiments rather than intuitions. Hold the model mix
and output count stable while changing one prompt group at a time.

For each run, measure:

- user-rated `strong`, `good`, `intriguing`, `meh`, and `reject` counts;
- causal-signature duplication within the batch and against the ledger;
- plain-language coherence failures;
- hard-constraint violations;
- the number of ideas worth a full dossier;
- total prompt and output length as secondary efficiency measures.

The representative eval set should include at least:

- one open central-conflict generation task;
- one repair task for a promising but flawed candidate;
- one novelty screen against the historical ledger;
- one first-ten-hours expansion;
- one ending-space expansion.

Do not infer that a larger panel is better. More independent samples may improve
coverage; dense communication and shared authority can instead force convergence.

## Immediate next sequence

1. Discuss and provisionally answer the seven minimum-world anchors.
2. Condense those answers and existing canon into a one-page positive brief.
3. Run a small controlled ideation experiment before another large panel.
4. Update the motif ledger and prompt based on measured failures, one change at a
   time.
5. Only then commission full dossiers and judging.

The pause before another large wave is intentional. It is not a demand to finish
Maldirin before finding its conflict; it is enough structure to ensure the next
prime mover belongs to the same world as every other candidate.
