# Magic: sources, methods, and consequences

This document records Maldirin's current governing model for magic. The
primordial foundation is established in
[Cosmogenesis](cosmogenesis.md), while the descent from that foundation to the
Vast, Gods, worlds, Maldirin, and particular arts remains open. The model
therefore defines what authored magic must account for without claiming that
every culture, practitioner, or God explains those facts in the same way.

## Established design constraints

- Magic is uncommon. Reliable practice requires unusual aptitude, training,
  alteration, patronage, inheritance, equipment, or some combination of them.
- Necromancy is a rare subset of magic, not Maldirin's universal supernatural
  grammar. A beginning necromancer is still an exceptional person.
- Non-necromantic practitioners exist. Their arts may work through bodies,
  perception, weather, craft, heat, water, distance, divinity, language, living
  processes, or matters not yet established. These are examples, not a closed
  list of schools.
- Magic belongs to Maldirin. It is not merely foreign weirdness leaking into an
  otherwise mundane world, and an ordinary encounter does not have to become
  haunted before a practitioner can act.
- There is no universal occult fuel, mandatory environmental grammar, or one
  hidden relationship beneath every art. The player's Spirit pool is a useful
  abstraction of personal occult exertion, not proof that every practitioner
  consumes the same substance.
- Player characters, allies, enemies, and other inhabitants obey the same
  authored rules. NPC importance does not excuse a missing tool, exhausted
  capacity, broken body, absent patron, or other real limit.
- Ordinary labor and material causes remain important. Magic does not make
  roads, food, medicine, engineering, authority, or skilled hands obsolete.

## Governing rule

**Magic has a source, a method, and consequences. Place matters when the power,
practitioner, target, or act gives it reason to matter.**

An authored capability should therefore establish:

1. **Effect:** what it can actually change.
2. **Basis:** what makes this practitioner or power capable of that change.
3. **Method:** what the actor does, uses, invokes, admits, alters, or endures.
4. **Cost and consequence:** what is spent, risked, damaged, attracted, changed,
   owed, or left behind.
5. **Dependencies and affinities:** which circumstances are truly necessary,
   which merely help or shape the act, and which do not matter.
6. **Limits and counters:** why the capability does not solve every problem and
   how other people can recognize, resist, interrupt, or outlast it.

Those facts may be only partly understood in-world. A community can preserve a
working method under a mistaken theology; a practitioner can know that iron
spoils an act without knowing why. The world model still requires a real
provenance for the result even when the player, character, or culture lacks it.

## Sources of magical agency

The following sources may overlap and do not form an exhaustive taxonomy:

- **the practitioner:** trained will, altered perception, bodily capacity,
  inherited disposition, memory, or another durable change to the self;
- **learned technique:** a repeatable operation whose competence travels with
  the practitioner even when its instruments do not;
- **Spirit or exertion:** bounded personal capacity to sustain an occult act;
- **materials and preparations:** reagents, charges, diagrams, instruments,
  crafted bodies, medicines, or other work performed beforehand;
- **artifacts and inherited works:** objects or structures with their own
  capacities, histories, maintenance, and failure modes;
- **other persons:** gods, Gods, dead initiative, bound entities, willing
  collaborators, patrons, hosts, or coerced participants;
- **relationships and offices:** kinship, service, membership, invitation,
  lawful standing, cult, oath, or another specific connection that the
  particular power can act through;
- **the target:** its body, history, injuries, substance, identity, present
  condition, or prior exposure;
- **collective action:** synchronized labor, ceremony, witness, song, formation,
  or another capacity no participant possesses alone;
- **time and circumstance:** season, death, birth, eclipse, repetition,
  emergency, transition, or a singular opportunity; and
- **place and environment:** terrain, weather, current, hearth, grave, route,
  jurisdiction, ecology, ruin, or a locally present power.

A source is not automatically a permission check. A Medium carries a changed
capacity to admit the dead. A Renderer carries learned processes and prepared
matter. A water-worker might carry an instrument that produces modest effects
anywhere, work far more strongly inside a maintained catchment, or belong to a
tradition whose power truly cannot leave one river. Each is legitimate when it
is authored deliberately.

## Portability and place

Portable agency is the ordinary expectation for player practices and for many
specialists whose lives involve travel, violence, service, or uncertain work.
It need not mean equal strength everywhere. Equipment can break, preparations
can run out, bodies can tire, patrons can refuse, targets can resist, and an art
can lose its favored circumstances.

Environmental and situational facts can play five different roles:

- **essential:** without the fact, this particular act cannot occur;
- **substitutable:** a carried preparation, different participant, greater
  cost, or setup action can replace it;
- **amplifying:** the act remains valid but gains reach, force, reliability,
  duration, efficiency, or precision;
- **shaping or resisting:** the fact changes the effect, opens a branch, creates
  collateral consequences, or provides a counter; or
- **irrelevant:** it has no mechanical bearing on the act.

Hard environmental gates should be uncommon and identity-defining. Soft
affinities, material interactions, tactical opportunities, and resistance are
more common. No capability acquires a site requirement merely because another
kind of magic has one.

Sites remain mechanically consequential. Breaking a sluice, carrying a hearth
coal, moving a body beyond consecrated ground, reaching a forge, exposing a
sealed threshold, or entering a God's manifestation can change available
choices. These are authored facts about those powers and actions, not evidence
that environment is Maldirin's master magical system.

## Gods and Gods

Gods are not ordinary practitioners with larger capability lists. Their agency
is inherent, personal, and supernatural. A god may exceed an ordinary mortal in
durability, reach, simultaneity, authority over its own nature, and power to
change the lives and material world around it. Gods need not share one origin:
some may be enlarged ancestors, awakened places, foreign persons, living powers,
inherited offices, makers of peoples, or beings for which the available
categories are inadequate.

Some gods are local because their personhood is a hearth, river, bridge, species,
city, lineage, storm, or other bounded existence. Such a god may be nearly
sovereign within that existence rather than merely supplying a useful modifier.
Other gods travel, divide themselves, abandon worshippers, or act wherever their
own reach permits. Locality is an individual truth about a god, not the definition
of godhood.

Worship does not create a god merely by believing hard enough, and a god is not
only a reservoir of magical energy. Devotion, maintenance, habitation, and
custom may help a god understand mortals, coordinate action, preserve a body,
extend a particular participation, or endure in a chosen form. Unless that
dependence belongs specifically to the god, neither worship nor a site gives it
permission to exist or act.

Maldirin also contains cosmological **Gods**. The distinction is qualitative,
not merely a measure of strength. A God may possess agency across lands, ages,
worlds, species, or orders of reality. It may make or transform gods, peoples,
climates, geographies, inherited laws, or modes of existence. A local body,
cult, office, voice, dream, or manifestation can be one participation in a much
greater person rather than the source of that person's power.

An aspect, adherent, body, instrument, agreement, or site may give a God
precision, intimacy, continuity, leverage, safety, or an exposed form through
which lesser beings can answer it. These are interfaces and vulnerabilities,
not universal batteries. A God may still impose consequences upon people who
never worshipped it and act where it has no temple.

Gods are not necessarily omnipotent. Their limits should be commensurate with
their scale: rival Gods, incompatible orders, divided attention, contradiction
within their own nature, an ancient defeat imposed by something comparable,
self-binding commitments, or the catastrophic consequences of acting at full
magnitude. A God may also be wholly incapable of an act alien to what it is.
Absence of a convenient local provider should not be the default explanation.

Mortals may defeat an aspect, frustrate an intervention, sever an army from its
divine commander, exploit conflict among manifestations, enlist a rival, or
make action too costly. Such victories can permanently change what a God can
accomplish in Maldirin without implying that a mortal struck down its totality.
A merely local god may be destroyable, but even then the deed should reflect the
god's actual body and magnitude rather than treating it as a large magician.

This is a working account of divine operation, not a claim that every culture
agrees on what gods or Gods are, recognizes the distinction, or uses the same
capitalization.

## Necromancy and other practices

Necromancers bring trained capacities, preparations, relationships, altered
bodies, and knowledge of death into ordinary places. Different practices may
host dead initiative, maintain a prepared corpse, coordinate external dead,
transform death-altered matter, alter anatomy, or do something not yet designed.
They need not share one mechanism merely because societies call all of them
necromancy.

The same breadth applies outside necromancy. A healer may extend a living
process through trained perception and touch; a heat-worker may carry a prepared
instrument; a shrine practitioner may depend on one god's office; an inherited
lineage may possess an internal faculty that functions wherever its bearer can
survive. Some arts are teachable, some transmissible only through alteration or
relationship, and some not reproducible by mortals at all.

Practice identity should come from what the player repeatedly manipulates and
risks, not from a generic list of conditions. Environment can enrich, challenge,
or define an individual tradition without supplying every class with the same
setup loop.

## Population and procedural generation

People are not generated as random collections of specialties. Regions,
societies, institutions, routes, occupations, patrons, training lineages, and
material access orient who is likely to be present. An appropriate specialist
encountered in ordinary circumstances should usually possess the bodily
capacity, knowledge, tools, preparations, collaborators, and favored conditions
that make their competence credible.

A region is an orientation, not a uniform modifier. Localities within it differ
in infrastructure, routes, land, institutions, inherited works, supernatural
presence, and degradation. Those differences affect which practitioners settle
there, what they can prepare, which patrons can act, and how strongly some arts
perform. They do not make every capability a derivative of the locality.

Exceptions need provenance: travel, migration, exile, captivity, emergency
deployment, deliberate concealment, lost equipment, bodily injury, exhausted
preparations, a disrupted patron, or another authored pressure. A deprived
specialist may use a fallback, seek materials or access, substitute greater
cost, change tactics, bargain, withdraw, or accept disadvantage. Procedural
generation must not erase the limitation with an unexplained coefficient, nor
invent an environmental dependency simply to make a specialist feel local.

The shared rules should model capability requirements, costs, preparations,
affinities, opportunities, and counters generically. Region and encounter packs
may add new facts and reactions without declaring today's examples to be an
exhaustive cosmology.

## Still open

- How Maldirin, the Vast, gods, Gods, and settled orders descend from or relate
  to the First's gift of genuine otherness.
- Whether Maldirin names a world, a larger reality, or both in different usage.
- Which broad kinds of magic particular cultures recognize, and how their
  taxonomies conflict with practice rather than merely using different labels.
- Which capacities can be taught, inherited, granted, made, stolen, awakened,
  or only encountered.
- How Spirit should relate mechanically to practices whose primary source is an
  artifact, collective, patron, altered body, or external formation.
- Which gods, practices, regions, and magical examples become canon rather than
  useful prototypes.
