# Necromancer — Class, Practice, and World Integration

> Independent design consultation produced with Claude Fable 5 on 2026-07-26.
> The recommendations below are proposals, not established canon.
>
> Editorial note: Fable’s brief did not define Ward, so the model flagged it as
> an assumption. In the current implementation Ward is a capped defensive
> resource that absorbs incoming damage. The Medium critique should be read
> with that correction.

## Executive recommendation

Keep and deepen the Medium and the material-specialist core of the
Thaumaturgist. Dissolve the current Summoner as a standalone practice unless
persistent-ally engineering is out of scope: its stated concerns of consent,
command, and identity are not expressed by a disposable, battle-duration shade.
Move negotiated identity into the Medium, and move command of embodied dead into
a persistent corpse-working practice.

Adopt a five-practice target:

1. **Medium** — hosts named dead in the living body.
2. **Bonebinder** — seats initiative in prepared remains and maintains a
   persistent corporeal servant.
3. **Renderer** — extracts, prepares, and applies occult properties from
   material remains.
4. **Nomenclator** — works through names, vows, debts, and witnessed terms.
5. **Ferryman** — governs thresholds, passage, refuge, and the proper conveyance
   of the dead.

The Ferryman should ship last because it depends most directly on cosmology that
remains provisional. A four-practice fallback can retain a heavily rebuilt
Unquiet Host in place of the Bonebinder and defer the Ferryman.

Use **practice**, not class or tradition, as the player-facing ontology.
*Necromancer* is the shared identity and body of universal craft. A practice is
the method by which that necromancer works.

Before implementing a broader roster, establish one cost doctrine:

> Spirit normally pays for occult action. Damage to Health, body condition, or
> wounds marks an overdraw, broken term, or other transgression; it is not a
> routine second mana bar.

This keeps Spirit meaningful and preserves wounds as consequential events rather
than fuel counters.

## Design principles

### One owned verb

Each practice should own a verb no other practice performs: *host*, *bind*,
*render*, *name*, or *convey*. If two practices both reduce to “spend Spirit to
deal occult damage,” their difference is cosmetic.

### Differentiation must survive prose deletion

Remove names and flavor text. A practice should still differ in:

- the substrate on which it acts;
- when and how it pays costs;
- its relation to bodies, wounds, and equipment;
- what work it can perform;
- what evidence it leaves;
- which people seek, tolerate, or reject it;
- what information it can obtain;
- what routes it opens and closes;
- how its failure creates further story.

### The dead remain people

The setting treats survival after death as disputed, names as durable holds, and
consent as consequential. Repeatable mechanics that summon, expend, and forget
the dead teach the player that shades are ammunition, regardless of what the
prose claims. Named dead, remembered terms, and persistent consequences should
make personhood mechanically visible.

### Social position is mechanical

The cultural spectrum from reverence to revulsion should be playable rather than
decorative. Different practices should receive different requests, access,
prices, scrutiny, and prohibitions. “All necromancers are equally feared” would
erase one of the setting’s strongest premises.

### Economies differ structurally

Distinct resource colors are insufficient. Practices should differ in the shape
of expenditure:

- capacity and maintenance;
- standing upkeep;
- preparation and banking;
- information-based discounts;
- large discrete tolls.

### Information is a signature capability

In a campaign where objectives can be satisfied through testimony, evidence,
maps, records, memories, and investigation, information access can distinguish
practices more deeply than combat actions.

### Evidence is an output

Every practice should answer “what did the necromancer leave behind?”
differently: hosting scars and testimony, a walking body, chemical residue,
sealed terms, or a disturbed threshold.

## Roster-worthiness rubric

Score a candidate from zero to two on each axis. A practice should have no zero
and should score at least eleven of sixteen. If two candidates duplicate both
their verb and their economy, merge them regardless of prose.

| Axis | Test |
|---|---|
| Verb | Does it own an action no other practice performs? |
| Locus | Does it act on a distinct substrate: self, corpse, matter, name, or passage? |
| Economy | Is its expenditure cadence structurally distinct? |
| Body | Does it use wounds, condition, and recovery differently? |
| Information | Can it produce knowledge or evidence no one else can? |
| Social | Does it occupy a distinct cultural and institutional position? |
| Routes | Does it open and close different campaign edges? |
| Failure | Does failure create a distinctive, playable consequence? |

## Critique of the current prototypes

### Medium

The Medium has the strongest social fantasy in the prototype: last words,
identification, disputed deaths, testimony, and the fear of possession or false
testimony. That is specific to necromancy and naturally supports Perception and
Persuasion.

The combat kit does not yet express it. Spirit Lash is a generic occult attack,
while Borrowed Might draws on an undifferentiated dead strength. The important
question is not merely that a dead person was borrowed, but **who** was admitted
and under what terms.

Borrowed Might’s wound cost is useful friction but currently flat. A soldier,
midwife, mason, murderer, or drowned child should strain the host differently.
A guest admitted under terms should not cost the same as a dead initiative
forced across the threshold.

Keep the Medium, but rebuild it around a small roster of named guests whose
identity changes combat, information access, costs, and social consequences.
Do not let Charisma make the Medium the only practice capable of meaningful
social play.

### Summoner

A second combat actor is mechanically distinctive. It changes targeting, action
economy, defense, and battlefield control in ways a self-buff cannot. The
themes of command, consent, identity, and familiar bodies also belong in this
setting.

The present mechanics contradict those themes. A shade raised for several
rounds, expended, and forgotten is a disposable-minion pattern. Consent and
identity are prose laid over ammunition.

The Summoner also overlaps the Medium metaphysically—dead initiative is hosted
in the self or in temporary air—and its healing/damage economy overlaps the
Medium’s use of bodily cost. Strength and Vitality then distinguish it mainly
as “the bruiser,” which is a weak reason for roster space.

Recommended disposition:

- negotiated identity and guest terms move to the Medium;
- command of a dead body becomes the Bonebinder;
- temporary shades become a later Bonebinder technique;
- Soul Tithe is cut or wholly redesigned because routine Health exchange
  weakens the Spirit economy.

If a persistent companion is too expensive to implement, the Unquiet Host can
remain, but every recurring shade should be named, remember prior summonings,
have explicit terms, and accumulate obligations.

### Thaumaturgist

This prototype has the strongest distinct economy: tools, harvesting, quality,
provenance, legality, preparation, and services. It connects naturally to
apothecary work, preservation, surgery, markets, and forensic evidence.

The name is too broad. Thaumaturgy means wonder-working generally, while this
practice is corpse chemistry and rendering. **Renderer** is more specific,
material, and occupational.

Grave Salt plus Corpsefire is a conventional alchemist combat kit. The practice
only earns its place if provenance is load-bearing:

- source and ownership affect legality and use;
- harvesting skill changes quality and yield;
- preparation is constrained;
- spent mixtures leave identifiable residue;
- Medicine grants qualitative surgical and forensic actions rather than only a
  larger check value.

Avoid both stockpile abundance, which trivializes scarcity, and severe
consumable scarcity, which makes players hoard every interesting action.
Preparation slots are a stronger foundation than unlimited inventory stacks.

### Cross-cutting problems

- All three prototypes converge on occult damage.
- No prototype owns names, vows, or obligations despite their importance to
  Maldirin’s metaphysics.
- Universal body and occult branches are sound, provided they contain shared
  craft rather than signature practice abilities.
- Starting kits are concrete, but equipment relationships should also differ:
  tools that degrade, vestments that identify a practitioner, repair supplies,
  incriminating instruments, and objects legal or illegal to carry.

## Recommended practices

### Medium — street name: speaker

**Core fantasy:** the body is a venue in which named dead may speak and act.

**Method:** bounded hosting under literal guest-right. A guest received under
terms is constrained by them; one admitted carelessly is not.

**Combat loop:** maintain a small ledger of guests. Each guest supplies a
stance, technique, defense, or damage register. A soldier may grant weapon
competence, a drowned witness presence and control, and a dead rival precision
at an unpleasant price. Spirit Lash remains an unhosted baseline. Borrowed Might
becomes a deeper draw on the current guest, with a guest-specific wound risk.

**Body and equipment:** hosting creates strain and legible scars. Practitioners
can sometimes read who has occupied a Medium. Gravecloth is recognizable
vestment—proof of legitimacy in one place and cause for exclusion in another.

**Economy:** Spirit represents hosting capacity. Admission and maintenance
consume Spirit; bodily harm occurs when terms are exceeded or a dangerous guest
is accepted.

**Mundane work:** testimony, identification, last words, disputed inheritance,
and locating knowledge held by a dead person. Perception and Persuasion lead;
Medicine can benefit situationally from hosted expertise.

**Provenance:** personal effects and reliable particulars establish contact.
The relevant provenance is that of a person rather than an item.

**Social consequence:** courts and households seek Mediums while doubting the
truth of their testimony. False testimony, suspected possession, and an
unwelcome guest create specific legal and religious reactions.

**Information:** what a dead person says—partial, motivated, and possibly false.

**Routes:** opens testimony and negotiation; may close consecrated or private
spaces that reject anyone carrying the dead.

**Weaknesses and failure:** modest while unhosted; guest agendas; damaged trust;
an occupant who overstays.

**Advancement:** additional guests, stronger and longer hosting, residual
impressions, projection from an emptied vessel, or nearly perfect guest terms.

### Bonebinder — street name: corpse-keeper

**Core fantasy:** a dead body remembers its work; the practitioner raises,
maintains, and answers for it.

**Method:** seat a continuity—not necessarily the original person—inside
prepared remains. The remains retain bodily history and learned habit.

**Combat loop:** one persistent servitor with full anatomy. It supplies physical
damage, interception, carrying, and battlefield labor. The player directs,
supports, repairs, and fights competently with ordinary weapons. Temporary
shades can appear later as risky battlefield reinforcements.

**Body and equipment:** the servant is repaired rather than healed. Pitch,
sinew, salt, wire, and replacement material support a distinct maintenance
economy. Destruction of the servant can wound the binder through the tether.
The servant’s scars form a persistent history.

**Economy:** Spirit is committed as upkeep while the servant remains active,
with larger expenditure on raising and major repair.

**Mundane work:** Athletics and Survival; hauling, digging, holding, keeping
watch, and using remembered trade skills.

**Provenance:** ownership and identity of a body are central. A surrendered,
hostile, ancient, executed, stolen, or recognized body creates different rights
and consequences.

**Social consequence:** this is the clearest revulsion pole. The evidence walks
beside the player. Settlements may refuse entry, while isolated communities,
roads, ruins, and war work may make the practice invaluable.

**Information:** what the body remembers somatically—habit, trained motion, and
recognition without spoken testimony.

**Routes:** opens heavy-labor, wilderness, siege, and dangerous-travel edges;
closes many respectable social spaces.

**Weaknesses and failure:** dependency on one vulnerable companion, material
maintenance, tether backlash, and severe social exclusion. Recognition of the
body by family or former associates should be a recurring authored risk.

**Advancement:** improved command, repairs, remembered trades, alternate bodies,
brief shade reinforcements, and a late fork between preserving individual
identity or building a more capable composite.

### Renderer — street name: salt-boiler

**Core fantasy:** death leaves useful properties in matter; careful work can
separate, preserve, and apply them.

**Method:** occult chemistry, rendering, preservation, and surgery.

**Combat loop:** prepare a limited set of mixtures before danger. Grave salts
weaken, corpsefire burns, coagulants control bleeding, and solvents alter armor
or materiality. A sickle remains a credible ordinary weapon.

**Body and equipment:** burns, stained hands, damaged lungs, and chemical
exposure make the work readable on the practitioner. Conversely, this is the
practice with specialist surgical treatment and cause-of-death forensics.

**Economy:** Spirit is banked into preparations rather than spent only at the
moment of use. Preparation slots and material quality prevent both abundance and
hoarding paralysis.

**Mundane work:** Medicine, Barter, and field harvesting through Survival.

**Provenance:** every important reagent has source, ownership, quality, and
legal status. Spent preparations leave forensic residue linked to method and
possibly maker.

**Social consequence:** pragmatic tolerance. Communities want an apothecary,
preserver, or surgeon until harvesting, contamination, or contraband becomes
visible. Guilds, physicians, brokers, and authorities all have distinct reasons
to regulate the practice.

**Information:** what matter proves—cause and time of death, contamination,
poison, origin, and occult alteration.

**Routes:** opens markets, guilds, surgeries, smuggling, and harvesting
opportunities. It rarely hard-closes a route, but creates accumulating legal and
evidentiary pressure.

**Weaknesses and failure:** preparation dependence, material starvation,
evidence trails, and temptation toward more effective but less defensible
sources. Ordinary mixtures should be weak against incorporeal entities.

**Advancement:** additional preparations, safer field work, rare yields,
forensic mastery, preservation that arrests death, or reduction capable of
affecting immaterial forms.

### Nomenclator — street name: grave-clerk

**Core fantasy:** debts outlive debtors; what is truly named can be held to
terms.

**Method:** true particulars, witnessed vows, obligations, seals, and names.

**Combat loop:** control scales with knowledge. An unnamed manifestation can be
briefly checked; a named one can be bound, turned, released, or unmade. Living
targets require a true particular such as a name, crime, or debt. Raw damage is
deliberately poor; the practitioner carries a real weapon.

**Body and equipment:** sworn wounds do not close until their associated
obligation is settled. The register, seal, ink, and cord are both tools and
highly incriminating evidence.

**Economy:** actions are expensive at baseline and discounted by verified
particulars. Investigation is therefore combat preparation.

**Mundane work:** Persuasion in the language of terms, Perception, Barter,
probate, debt settlement, records, and releases.

**Provenance:** witnessed instruments and reliable records matter. People and
their cooperation become material components without becoming inventory items.

**Social consequence:** institutions fear this practice more than crowds do.
Courts, temples, creditors, and criminal networks all want access and monopoly.
False binding and falsified records are distinct accusations.

**Information:** documentary identity—registries, genealogies, debts, vows, and
true names.

**Routes:** opens archives, courts, chanceries, and negotiated passage through
bounded supernatural situations. It creates mortal enemies among people who
depend on anonymity or repudiated obligations.

**Weaknesses and failure:** weak against the truly anonymous, slow to acquire
power, and catastrophically vulnerable to the practitioner’s own broken vows.

**Advancement:** remote binding, deeper registers, perception of unsettled
obligation, erasure of a name, or multi-party compacts capable of enforcing
peace between living institutions and the dead.

### Ferryman — street name: lantern

**Core fantasy:** the dead require conveyance; the practitioner knows how to
open, guard, and close the roads they travel.

**Method:** thresholds, passage, refuge, and the geography between durable
continuity and dissolution.

**Combat loop:** space control and banishment rather than direct destruction.
Thresholds deny movement, open a way beneath an incorporeal enemy, secure
retreat, or close pursuit. This is the strongest support for pacifist and
noncombat play.

**Body and equipment:** crossing costs appear as cold wounds or “left-behind”
losses that can be recovered by returning to a threshold. Lantern and staff are
recognized signs of safe conduct in some cultures.

**Economy:** large discrete Spirit tolls to open or close passages, with cheap
maintenance between them.

**Mundane work:** Survival, Perception, grief work, funeral escort, and route
craft.

**Provenance:** payment comes from funerals, escorts, laying-to-rest, and guiding
travel through unsafe country rather than harvesting bodies.

**Social consequence:** the reverence pole. Ferrymen may be welcomed where a
Bonebinder is attacked, though their arrival still announces death and those
who profit from hauntings oppose them.

**Information:** where the dead go, which crossings remain open, and where the
Marching Country or another incursion has weakened continuity.

**Routes:** opens crossings, sanctuaries, and safe camps; reads route pressure
and bypasses barriers that stop other practices.

**Weaknesses and failure:** low sustained damage, obligations to convey that
conflict with immediate goals, and disastrous consequences when a door is
opened but not closed.

**Advancement:** wider thresholds, carrying the living across, permanent refuge,
or travel on the far side. Defer this practice until its cosmology can be
supported consistently across regions.

## Comparison matrix

| Practice | Verb / locus | Combat | Spirit economy | Body relation | Unique knowledge | Social band |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Medium | host / self | guest stances and overdraw | capacity and maintenance | hosting strain | what the dead say | sought and mistrusted |
| Bonebinder | bind / corpse | persistent physical ally | standing upkeep | repair and tether backlash | what bodies remember | openly reviled |
| Renderer | render / matter | prepared mixtures | banked preparation | exposure and surgery | what matter proves | pragmatically tolerated |
| Nomenclator | name / obligation | knowledge-gated control | particular-based discount | sworn wounds | what records bind | institutionally feared |
| Ferryman | convey / threshold | denial and banishment | discrete tolls | crossing loss | where the dead go | often revered |

No two recommended practices share both a verb and an economy. Together they
cover mundane, occult, chemical, physical, spatial, and investigative play.

## Rename, merge, cut, and defer

- Rename **Thaumaturgist** to **Renderer**.
- Keep **Medium**, but make named guests and terms its mechanical center.
- Retire **Summoner** as a practice; distribute its strongest ideas between
  Medium and Bonebinder.
- Preserve **The Unquiet Host** as an epithet if useful.
- Cut routine flat Health costs from signature actions.
- Defer **Ferryman** until the cosmology of thresholds and the Marching Country
  is stable enough to support it outside a single region.
- Defer unsatisfiable sworn-wound edge cases until the obligation model exists.

### Credible lower-cost alternative

Ship four practices:

1. revised Medium;
2. rebuilt Unquiet Host;
3. Renderer;
4. Nomenclator.

In this version every summoned shade is named, remembers prior uses, has terms,
and accumulates obligation. It avoids persistent-servitor state, settlement
access rules, and full companion anatomy. The cost is a permanent overlap
between hosting and summoning, loss of the mundane physical-damage practice, and
weaker representation of social revulsion.

Choose this only if a persistent ally is genuinely unaffordable in the intended
development horizon.

## Player-facing ontology

Use **practice**.

- *Class* is mechanically clear but imports an abstract build-game vocabulary
  into a setting that treats magic as labor.
- *Tradition* implies lineage or orthodoxy and is better reserved for schools,
  regional teachings, and rival doctrines within a practice.
- *Trade* fits the world but conflicts with markets and Barter.
- *Practice* implies labor, repetition, method, and professional identity.

The player is a **Necromancer**. That identity owns universal craft and
advancement: corpse handling, spirit sight, rites, grave law, bodily endurance,
and occult capacity. The selected **practice** owns signature state, actions,
and advancement.

Cross-practice study should be a scarce, consequential second apprenticeship,
if it exists at all. Making every practice freely multiclassable would erase
the distinctions the roster is intended to create.

Formal names and street names can carry cultural variation. An institution may
say *Nomenclator* where a village says *grave-clerk*. This reinforces Maldirin’s
attention to names without pretending that a professional title is itself a
true name.

## Implementation implications

### Typed practice definitions

Add a `PracticeDefinition` registry with:

- stable id;
- formal and street names;
- starting attributes and skills;
- starting kit references;
- granted action ids;
- advancement-tree references;
- social tags;
- capability ids.

The practice registry grants rules by reference. It should not contain
executable prose branches.

### Actions own effects

Player actions should be typed definitions composed from effect primitives:
damage, body trauma, wound creation, resource change, status, ally operation,
threshold operation, evidence, knowledge, or obligation.

Practices grant action ids. Resolution should not switch on practice id.
Practice-specific modifiers read typed practice state. Narration stays in the
existing action narration catalog with explicit aliases and coverage tests.

### Capability predicates, not practice checks

Content should ask:

- can this character hear last words?
- can they read material evidence?
- do they travel with a visible servant?
- do they hold a registered name?
- can they open or close a threshold?

It should not ask whether `practiceId === "medium"` unless the identity itself
is the fictional fact at issue. Capabilities let items, guests, relationships,
or future practices satisfy existing content without rewriting it.

Story validation should prove that every required objective has viable
approaches for every launch practice. No single practice must be able to take
every route, but none should be unable to complete the campaign.

### Practice-scoped state

Keep namespaced persisted state:

- Medium guests and terms;
- Bonebinder servant identity, anatomy, provenance, and repair state;
- Renderer preparations and recipes;
- Nomenclator particulars, instruments, and sworn obligations;
- Ferryman thresholds and crossing obligations.

Each state block needs its own validation and normalization. Existing saves
receive the corresponding practice id and safe defaults. Unknown namespaced
fields should not be destroyed gratuitously during migration.

### Allies reuse existing anatomy

A persistent servant should be a combatant with ordinary anatomy, wounds, and
body condition. Repair is a recovery rule, not a parallel health system. This
reuses the project’s strongest mechanical depth while allowing a different
fictional relationship to recovery.

### Knowledge claims

Medium, Renderer, and Nomenclator all require knowledge to have:

- subject;
- source;
- confidence or verification state;
- evidence references;
- what the player knows;
- which factions know or believe it.

Build this claim vocabulary before hard-coding testimony, forensic proof, or
true particulars as isolated flags.

### Provenance across persons, bodies, and goods

The same conceptual model should serve:

- an item made from identified remains;
- a body claimed by a household;
- a personal effect used to contact a dead person;
- a record tied to a use-name;
- a chemical residue linked to a practitioner.

Do not force all of these into inventory lots, but give them shared source,
ownership, evidence, and knowledge references.

### Testing

Each practice needs contract tests for:

- action and narration catalog completeness;
- route-objective reachability;
- capability predicate behavior;
- save/load and migration;
- deterministic seeded outcomes;
- economy bounds and resource solvency;
- distinctive failure consequences;
- absence of illegal practice-id control flow in generic resolution.

### Staged rollout

1. Establish the practice registry and capability predicates by migrating the
   Medium without changing the campaign.
2. Build the Renderer to prove preparation, provenance, services, evidence, and
   faction knowledge.
3. Build the Bonebinder to prove persistent ally state and anatomy reuse.
4. Build the Nomenclator after claims and obligations exist end to end.
5. Build the Ferryman after the cosmology and regional route graph can support
   threshold play beyond one bespoke sequence.

## Immediate design decisions

1. Choose the five-practice roster or the four-practice lower-cost alternative.
2. Ratify or reject the Spirit/body-cost doctrine.
3. Decide whether persistent allies are in the near-term scope.
4. Ratify “practice” and formal/street name duality.
5. Define the minimum knowledge-claim model.
6. Define shared provenance across goods, bodies, personal effects, and records.
7. Decide how much consent and guest terms are mechanized at first release.
8. Decide whether the opening slice is retrofitted for all practices or becomes
   explicitly a Medium-only proving ground during staged development.

## Unresolved questions

- What happens when a sworn wound’s obligation becomes impossible to satisfy?
- Can a Bonebinder’s servant be a specific authored dead person, and does the
  route or the practice own that character’s content?
- How directly can the Marching Country affect hosted guests or registered
  names?
- What do rival practitioners of the same practice owe, teach, or deny one
  another?
- Which communities use the formal name, street name, or a third local name for
  each practice?
- Is reliable testimony possible, and if so, what separates it from compelled
  speech or a dead person’s sincere mistake?
- Can a practice lose access to its own signature capability through wounds,
  evidence seizure, broken terms, or faction action without making the run
  nonviable?
