# Scene variation catalog

Status: noncanonical scene proposals and authoring coverage targets.

The current proving ground already covers roadside burial labor, a surgeon's
awning, a mourning caravan, a custody dispute, road damage, missing wagons,
local authority, reliquary trade, and several kinds of hostile dead. This
catalog deliberately reaches beyond those situations. Its titles, regions,
institutions, and rewards are placeholders until Maldirin's geography is
settled.

The purpose is not to install every entry. It is to identify scene structures
that can carry world state, ordinary life, exchange, and recurrence without
turning a campaign into a fixed road or a shop circuit.

## Coverage rules

A new scene should do more than change prose. Prefer scenes that alter at least
two of route, time, resources, condition, knowledge, another actor's knowledge,
standing, obligation, evidence, custody, service access, or a later cast.

Across a regional pool:

- Mundane, mixed, and supernatural situations all appear. The supernatural
  remains exceptional enough to disturb an established ordinary world.
- Friendly, neutral, and hostile describe starting posture, not guaranteed
  endings.
- Social, exploration, hazard, mystery, service, exchange, labor, and ritual
  structures all receive meaningful representation.
- Failure changes terms, costs, routes, relationships, or evidence. It does not
  merely close the interesting button and return the same choice.
- Optional actions revise the remaining copy and options. Resolved branches do
  not remain on screen as though every incompatible choice was completed.
- Immediate, knowable stakes are disclosed. Hidden reactions, exact future
  pressure, and an enemy's use of gained time remain hidden.
- Every playable practice can matter sometimes, but ordinary skills and plain
  choices frequently remain best.

## Exchange is a scene structure, not a universal shop

Items and services should move through institutions with reasons to want them.
Coin is one settlement mechanism among barter, commission, requisition,
hospitality, consignment, restitution, and obligation.

Use these exchange shapes:

| Shape | Provider or counterparty | What varies | Player tension |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Scarcity request | hospital, work crew, garrison, household | demanded item tags and urgency | keep the supply for later or answer a present need |
| Commission | smith, Renderer, surgeon, shrine artisan | input item, labor access, turnaround, result | preserve a unique item or transform it permanently |
| Consignment | broker, caravan factor, temple auction | demand, provenance, delay, exposure | accept less now or risk a later return |
| Restricted purchase | guild, shrine, authority store | standing, papers, knowledge, finite stock | spend social access and currency on one scarce preparation |
| Service barter | guide, healer, ferryman, translator | accepted goods, obligation, time | pay in useful gear, accept a favor, or take a worse route |
| Requisition | army, authority, desperate community | legitimacy, compensation, coercion | surrender goods, contest authority, conceal them, or negotiate terms |
| Restitution | household, injured party, custodian | claim and evidence | keep legal salvage or repair a relationship and relinquish it |
| Reverse market | specialist buyers with a present need | accepted kinds, quality, provenance | a high offer competes with equipment, crafting, evidence, or later access |

Barter should resolve once per transaction. Variable demand changes which goods
matter and on what terms; it should not invite travel between vendors for
riskless arbitrage. A sale can remove a future route, evidence object, crafting
ingredient, or useful tool. A service can be more valuable than its cash price.

## Priority scene concepts

The twelve entries marked **Priority** cover the broadest missing structures.
Each can support regional variants without becoming a universal template.

### 1. The dyehouse cough — Priority

- **Register / posture / mode:** mundane, neutral, medical mystery and labor
  dispute.
- **Situation:** workers in a riverside dyehouse collapse with blackened gums.
  The owner blames a grave upstream; the dyers blame a newly cheap mordant.
- **Approaches:** Medicine compares symptoms; Barter traces the supplier;
  Perception examines vats and payroll substitutions; the player can halt the
  works by force of testimony, help finish a time-sensitive batch, or leave the
  workers to choose whether wages outweigh exposure.
- **Failure forward:** an uncertain diagnosis still identifies one unsafe room,
  but the wrong public accusation hardens an actor's belief and moves the
  contaminated goods into another market.
- **Exchange:** the dyers can pay in coin, treated cloth, or protective work.
  Their clean solvent is also useful to a Renderer, forcing a keep-versus-relief
  decision.
- **Recurrence:** a worker, owner, or supplier returns separately according to
  who was believed and whether the batch traveled.

### 2. Water for the lower fields — Priority

- **Register / posture / mode:** mundane, neutral, public assembly and hazard.
- **Situation:** a failing reservoir gate can save the lower fields only by
  flooding winter stores and an old cemetery on higher ground.
- **Approaches:** Athletics and tools attempt a partial opening; Survival judges
  the coming rain; Persuasion structures consent; legal papers or local standing
  change who will accept responsibility. A Warden can protect a bounded portion,
  but cannot preserve every claim.
- **Failure forward:** the gate opens badly, saving one district while injuring
  workers and creating a repair obligation. Refusal leaves a later food scarcity
  rather than immediate combat.
- **Exchange:** households offer stores, labor, or future shelter for priority.
  A factor offers coin for a decision that protects estate acreage.
- **Route order:** earlier weather knowledge, a rescued engineer, or the sale of
  repair iron changes the possible compromise.

### 3. The recalled physic — Priority

- **Register / posture / mode:** mundane, friendly, service and reverse market.
- **Situation:** an apothecary learns that one batch of wound wash was cut with a
  contaminated spirit. The player may own a vial; other buyers are already on
  the road.
- **Approaches:** surrender the vial for replacement, keep it as evidence, help
  find buyers, quietly test it, or sell the warning to a rival authority.
- **Failure forward:** a failed trace locates one buyer too late but reveals the
  direction of the remaining batch. Using the wash may treat a wound while
  creating a different complication.
- **Exchange:** the apothecary offers replacement, treatment, or credit rather
  than a universal refund. Scarcity and standing alter which is available.
- **Recurrence:** the same provider's later trust depends on whether the player
  protected patients, reputation, evidence, or personal advantage.

### 4. The old iron under heat — Priority

- **Register / posture / mode:** mundane with occult material, neutral, commissioned
  service.
- **Situation:** an itinerant smith can rework one named or unique implement whose
  original form is failing. The work will destroy identifying marks and may
  release whatever history the metal still carries.
- **Approaches:** preserve and repair, permanently transform, commission a sheath
  or fitting instead, seek the original maker, or sell the piece to the smith's
  patron.
- **Failure forward:** inadequate appraisal produces a serviceable but altered
  result and a recoverable fragment, not a vanished item. Concealed provenance
  may later be recognized.
- **Exchange:** coin covers labor, suitable fuel improves the result, and a
  personal referral can substitute for either. The unique item's mechanical use,
  identity, evidence value, and price compete directly.
- **Recurrence:** the smith remembers the work and can identify the transformed
  item in another person's hand.

### 5. A name entered for the living — Priority

- **Register / posture / mode:** mundane, wary, administrative negotiation.
- **Situation:** a muster, quarantine, or city gate requires a stable identity
  and sponsor. The necromancer's use-name works, but the record will travel.
- **Approaches:** enter the use-name willingly, present a relationship or work
  order, barter for a guarantor, find an unrecorded entry, or accept delayed
  inspection.
- **Failure forward:** passage is granted under escort, a bond, or confiscation
  rather than simply refused. A false record creates evidence with an actual
  custodian.
- **Exchange:** a licensed advocate trades sponsorship for coin, a particular
  service, or later testimony. Good standing improves terms but does not erase
  the record.
- **Recurrence:** the clerk, sponsor, or escort can later vouch, accuse, summon,
  or ask the named character for help.

### 6. The broker's empty table — Priority

- **Register / posture / mode:** mundane, neutral, consignment and market
  intelligence.
- **Situation:** a specialist buyer has coin but none of the material currently
  demanded by an army, hospital, shrine, or public work. The player carries one
  item that could answer the shortage and another with uncertain provenance.
- **Approaches:** sell immediately, consign for a later premium, trade for a
  scarce service, disclose provenance, conceal it, or carry the goods to the
  institution that needs them.
- **Failure forward:** poor Barter secures a sale with worse terms or exposes the
  lot to another claimant. It never permits repeated rolls against the same
  offer.
- **Exchange:** demand is institution- and chapter-specific. The offer competes
  with equipment, evidence, practice preparation, and direct restitution.
- **Recurrence:** the buyer later reports who purchased the item, if their
  relationship and knowledge make disclosure credible.

### 7. The tenant's wake — Priority

- **Register / posture / mode:** mundane, friendly but divided, household social
  scene.
- **Situation:** a family holds a wake while two living heirs disagree about a
  lease, an apprenticeship, or a dependent's care. No dead testimony is required
  and inviting it may worsen the dispute.
- **Approaches:** examine ordinary documents, speak separately to kin, accept
  hospitality without intervening, offer neutral custody of one object, or use a
  practice only with explicit permission.
- **Failure forward:** the household makes a decision and remembers the player's
  role; uncertainty shifts an obligation or later claimant rather than blocking
  the route.
- **Exchange:** food, shelter, introductions, and a household tool are possible
  returns. Taking a valuable object can foreclose trust even if the transfer is
  lawful.
- **Recurrence:** different heirs return with distinct knowledge, standing, and
  claims.

### 8. The false corpse-worker — Priority

- **Register / posture / mode:** mundane deception with possible occult residue,
  initially hostile social confrontation.
- **Situation:** a traveling fraud has sold protection against a fabricated
  haunting. The charm is useless, but someone has since introduced a real danger
  to preserve the fraud.
- **Approaches:** expose the craft, identify the later intrusion, recruit the
  fraud's social knowledge, demand restitution, hand the matter to local
  authority, or leave before the crowd decides all practitioners are alike.
- **Failure forward:** exposure without proof scatters the gathering and turns the
  fraud into a mobile hostile witness. Quiet cooperation may stop the danger but
  leave the original lie intact.
- **Exchange:** recovered payments have claimants; the fraud owns useful mundane
  props and route knowledge. Keeping either carries different evidence.
- **Recurrence:** the fraud can become informant, rival, accused corpse, or a
  genuinely trained apprentice depending on consequence rather than a morality
  flag.

### 9. What the courier carried — Priority

- **Register / posture / mode:** mundane, neutral, exploration and evidence
  custody.
- **Situation:** a courier is found injured and separated from a locked dispatch
  case. Several institutions can plausibly claim the case; the courier can name
  only one person before losing consciousness.
- **Approaches:** treat first, recover the case, open it for immediate danger,
  deliver it sealed, seek the named person, or sell the route to an interested
  party.
- **Failure forward:** the courier lives but misremembers; the case arrives with
  broken custody; or another party obtains a copy. Facts and beliefs diverge
  without inventing an omniscient wanted score.
- **Exchange:** delivery earns coin, access, or a favor. The case's contents may
  be more valuable as evidence than loot, and opening it changes who will pay.
- **Recurrence:** courier, recipient, claimant, and intercepting party can all
  return under different terms.

### 10. The bell without a voice — Priority

- **Register / posture / mode:** mixed, neutral, civic mystery and exploration.
- **Situation:** a town bell still moves, but its sound no longer reaches one
  district. Work shifts, alarms, and burial hours have begun to fail. The cause
  may be stolen bronze, an acoustic fraud, a vacant divine office, or an
  unfinished-country boundary depending on the regional version.
- **Approaches:** inspect tower and streets, reconstruct the last audible route,
  substitute runners, renegotiate work schedules, repair the bell, or follow the
  lost sound where it has collected.
- **Failure forward:** a temporary warning system restores safety while the cause
  remains active. Pursuing the mystery costs the opportunity to prevent one
  ordinary loss.
- **Exchange:** guilds and households offer different payment for restoration,
  proof, or silence. Bell metal is useful and strongly claimed.
- **Variation:** only one cause is active per authored variant; do not present a
  procedurally blended riddle.

### 11. A god under borrowed rafters — Priority

- **Register / posture / mode:** supernatural, friendly but burdensome,
  hospitality and ritual.
- **Situation:** displaced people have housed a small refugee divinity in a
  granary, bathhouse, ship, or roadside chapel. Its old shelter is gone. Its
  presence protects one relationship while damaging the building and alarming
  local authority.
- **Approaches:** secure temporary guest-right, move the host object, negotiate a
  limit through its custodians, find another institution willing to receive it,
  expose an unacceptable demand, or refuse involvement.
- **Failure forward:** the god remains housed under harsher terms, moves without
  all of its people, or loses one function while retaining personhood. Failure
  does not automatically create a boss fight.
- **Exchange:** worshippers offer guides, refuge, or culturally specific goods;
  an authority offers coin for removal. The host object is valuable but cannot
  be treated as unclaimed loot.
- **Recurrence:** god, custodians, host institution, and displaced opponents can
  reappear independently.

### 12. The incorporated advocate — Priority

- **Register / posture / mode:** supernatural-political, courteous hostile,
  negotiation and public choice.
- **Situation:** an agent of the Marching Country arrives before an army and
  offers a real remedy to a local exclusion: released tenants, an opened border,
  or the end of a hereditary burden. Acceptance also makes one durable refuge or
  return contestable.
- **Approaches:** answer the grievance by ordinary reform, negotiate a bounded
  concession, expose what cannot return, protect the threatened continuity,
  divide the advocate's coalition, or accept incorporation and carry its cost.
- **Failure forward:** the offer wins one constituency rather than the entire
  place. The player can retreat with people, evidence, or a preserved route and
  confront the changed coalition later.
- **Exchange:** resources, protection, office, and safe passage may be offered;
  these are political terms rather than a disguised vendor inventory.
- **Recurrence:** the advocate remains culturally intact and remembers precise
  agreements, betrayals, and reforms.

## Recurring-actor structures

Recurrence should reveal changed roles, not repeat a store menu.

| First role | Later role | State that changes the scene |
| --- | --- | --- |
| patient | witness, employer, or claimant | treatment quality, confidentiality, and who paid |
| provider | accused practitioner or scarce expert | respect for their work, referral, legal pressure, and unpaid obligation |
| defeated opponent | prisoner, guide, dependent, or faction agent | cause of defeat, spared wounds, custody, and shared enemies |
| clerk or courier | custodian of evidence | use-name given, record opened, delivery completed, and standing |
| household host | displaced traveler | hospitality accepted, property taken, kin helped, and route pressure |
| broker | buyer's representative or informant | provenance disclosed, consignment outcome, and who learned of the lot |
| labor crew | organized constituency | time spent helping, injury caused, promised pay, and later public work |
| divine custodian | political envoy | guest-right terms, relocation, witnessed breach, and host institution |

An actor's return should normally change at least one of authority, need,
location, allies, or knowledge. Familiar dialogue alone is not recurrence.

## Context-sensitive variants

Vary a scene through explicit authored branches rather than interpolated mush:

- **Route order:** a provider has already heard the warning; a commodity has
  already been sold; a claimant arrives first; the player carries a relevant
  witness.
- **Condition:** a wounded player needs the offered service; an injured companion
  changes shelter and transport; low Spirit makes a preparation attractive but
  does not force its purchase.
- **Practice and capability:** show a sourced option when a practice changes the
  method. Do not hide ordinary solutions or give every practice one cosmetic
  line.
- **Items and provenance:** ownership, recognizable marks, contamination, and
  current demand affect terms separately from generic sale value.
- **Knowledge and belief:** the player may know a fact an actor rejects; an actor
  may believe a false report for supported reasons; evidence can change hands.
- **Relationships:** kinship, employment, custody, sponsorship, and obligation
  open different actions from positive standing.
- **Pressure:** weather, pursuit, scarcity, public fear, and a supernatural front
  change eligibility and cost without making every scene about the central plot.
- **Prior resolution:** after the player helps, onward copy acknowledges that the
  work is done. Incompatible conclusions disappear rather than display as
  completed together.

## Pool-level coverage matrix

Before expanding a region, score its authored pool rather than trusting tone to
create variety.

| Axis | Minimum useful spread |
| --- | --- |
| Register | at least half mundane; no more than one third overtly supernatural unless the region premise requires it |
| Starting posture | at least one friendly, two neutral, and one hostile noncombat situation |
| Structure | labor/hazard, social negotiation, service/exchange, exploration/mystery, and recurrence |
| Resolution | certain cost, seeded check, resource sacrifice, relationship/evidence choice, and refusal or withdrawal |
| Reward | resource, service, access, knowledge, relationship, route, and avoided harm across the pool; never all at once |
| Failure | injury, time, worse terms, partial truth, shifted custody, new evidence, lost opportunity, or changed later cast |
| Practice relevance | every practice receives authored leverage somewhere; no required objective depends on one practice |
| Economy | at least one keep-versus-sell decision and one service whose best price is not simply coin |

These are regional coverage targets, not quotas for every five-minute stretch.
The authored plot may tighten the distribution when a crisis warrants it, then
return the player to a world where people still work, trade, disagree, shelter,
and survive for reasons other than necromancy.
