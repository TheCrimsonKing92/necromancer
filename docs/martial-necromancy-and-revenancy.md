# Martial necromancy and Revenancy

Status: **provisional class and transformation design**. This document promotes
one fifth-seat candidate to focused proof and defines Revenancy as a rare world
achievement. Names, exact abilities, numerical balance, and unlock content are
not canon.

## Three different concepts

`Martial necromancer` can describe three different design layers. They should
not be collapsed merely because all may carry a weapon.

1. **Martial formation or background:** an order, office, military lineage, or
   personal history that teaches arms, armor, fieldcraft, and confrontation with
   hostile dead. A Medium, Bonebinder, Renderer, or Summoner can belong to it.
2. **A distinct mortality practice:** a starting-practice candidate whose
   necromantic loop changes martial play by manipulating the practitioner's own
   living functions and proximity to death.
3. **Revenancy:** a rare, dangerous transformation achieved during the persistent
   campaign. It retains and changes the character's original practice rather
   than erasing it.

The first supplies the broad warrior-mage fantasy. The second earns a roster seat
only if its repeated object remains distinct. The third is an exceptional world
event, not ordinary character creation.

## Cross-practice martial formation

A regional warrior-necromancer order can train members of several practices:

- a Medium yields a faculty to a dead duelist or lets a guest take a bounded
  reaction;
- a Bonebinder fights as one half of a coordinated pair;
- a Renderer commits coatings, charges, smoke, counters, and treated weapons;
- a Summoner directs a formation while personally holding a breach; and
- the mortality practitioner changes the state of their own living body.

This formation may grant Strength or Dexterity development, weapon and armor
access, battlefield Medicine, anti-manifestation training, shared equipment, and
practice-specific techniques. It should not become a second class taxonomy. Its
world identity follows the history, language, patrons, enemies, and institutions
that produced it.

## Fifth-seat candidate: the mortality practice

Working names such as `Last Vigil`, `Pale Discipline`, `Deathsworn`, or
`Graveblade` describe pieces of the fantasy but do not yet name the shared
practice. The design should settle the loop before the title.

### Repeated object

The practitioner manipulates **their own vital state**. They learn to arrest and
restore breath, circulation, sensation, pain, warmth, and the ordinary relation
between living initiative and the body without immediately surrendering that
initiative.

This is not:

- a Medium hosting someone else's initiative;
- a Bonebinder modifying another prepared body;
- a Renderer applying a consumable effect to the self;
- a generic warrior with death-colored attacks; or
- a Revenant who is already dead.

### Source, method, and consequences

- **Source:** an altered or trained capacity of the living practitioner, perhaps
  initiated through severe instruction, bodily ordeal, inherited aptitude, or a
  procedure not every person can survive.
- **Method:** deliberately suspend, deaden, expose, or recall particular living
  functions while acting through weapons, movement, breath, and anatomy.
- **Consequences:** hidden or accumulated injury, failed recall, reduced healing,
  vulnerability to effects that govern dead initiative, social recognition, and
  the possibility that a function does not fully return.
- **Affinities:** mortuary tools, practiced weapons, controlled preparation,
  certain remains, or death-altered sites may improve particular techniques.
  The baseline practice travels in the altered body.
- **Counters:** bodily destruction, forced circulation or breath, heat, hostile
  control of dead initiative, interruption during recall, and practice-specific
  enemies or materials. No one counter should apply universally without being
  earned by the world's metaphysics.

### Direct-play loop

The loop is **suspend, commit, fight, and return**:

1. Suspend one function or enter a bounded pale state.
2. Commit the resulting capability to movement, defense, perception, or an
   actual weapon technique.
3. Endure damage and state interactions that may be less visible while sensation
   or circulation is absent.
4. Recall the function at a tactically safe moment or risk permanent harm.

Possible techniques illustrate the space without establishing final actions:

- still breath and pulse to evade some living senses, cross poison or smoke, or
  become legible to particular dead;
- deaden an injured arm so it can continue gripping a weapon while further
  condition loss becomes harder to assess;
- let a hand and weapon partly leave ordinary life to strike an incorporeal
  manifestation;
- arrest bleeding until circulation returns, preserving immediate Health while
  carrying deferred danger;
- suppress pain to ignore a wound penalty without repairing the wound; and
- recall several functions at once, risking shock when accumulated consequences
  become authoritative.

The class must remain a martial character. Weapon properties, reach, accuracy,
armor, positioning, wounds, body parts, treatment, and retreat all matter. Its
necromancy changes those systems rather than replacing them with a spell tray.

### Opposing traditions

#### The Last Vigil

The practitioner preserves mortal integrity. They cross briefly, confront dead
and living enemies at weapon range, and regard the safe return of every function
as the discipline's proof. They are suited to orders that retrieve bodies,
oppose hostile dead, protect practitioners, or enter places ordinary soldiers
cannot survive.

Their criticism of the rival path: every function left dead teaches the body to
continue without its living owner.

#### The Pale Discipline

The practitioner permits functions to remain suspended, accepts lasting bodily
change, and treats complete return as increasingly optional. Longer commitment
creates more powerful or unusual states, but ordinary healing, sensation,
equipment, and social presentation become harder to preserve.

Their criticism of the Last Vigil: refusing durable change sacrifices power and
knowledge merely to preserve the appearance of an untouched life.

These names may ultimately describe traditions rather than the practice itself.
Neither doctrine is automatically right, and both can produce protectors,
predators, zealots, and hypocrites.

## Revenancy as an earned transformation

A Revenant retains its own initiative in its dead body. This should be a rare
campaign transformation available through several specific methods, not a
routine class choice or a universal recipe.

### General achievement shape

A viable route normally requires:

1. **Knowledge:** obtain a credible account of retained initiative by study,
   service, bargaining, defeating such a being, or another costly encounter.
2. **Method:** prepare a return appropriate to the character's practice and the
   discovered world facts.
3. **Continuance:** secure the body or another temporary means by which the
   departing initiative can remain coherent.
4. **Death:** undergo a death whose circumstances are controlled well enough for
   the chosen method to operate.
5. **Price:** surrender something irreversible rather than purchasing a pure
   upgrade.
6. **Risk:** failure remains actual character death. The campaign and successor
   system continue without guaranteeing another attempt.

The player need not see this as a checklist before discovering the relevant
knowledge. Once understood, requirements and risks should be communicated
fairly enough for an intentional attempt.

### Practice-specific routes

- A Medium might briefly receive their own departing initiative through another
  living host, then return to the prepared corpse.
- A Bonebinder might prepare their future corpse as the durable body they intend
  to occupy.
- A Renderer might arrest the transformations that normally destroy the body's
  capacity to receive its former initiative.
- A Summoner might hold the departing self within a prepared retinue until the
  body can answer again.
- A mortality practitioner might complete deliberately the crossing they have
  spent their discipline approaching.
- A God, artifact, precursor work, existing Revenant, or lich might impose a
  different route with different ownership and consequences.

These are methods, not proof that every practice can always become a Revenant.
Campaign facts determine which routes actually exist.

### Mechanical and social cost

Revenancy should change the whole character model:

- ordinary healing may cease or yield to repair and occult restoration;
- some living afflictions lose force while bodily destruction, fire, banishment,
  control, confinement, or particular predators become more dangerous;
- wounds and deterioration may persist until materially addressed;
- food, sleep, warmth, breath, Spirit, and rest may change without removing the
  campaign's resource pressure;
- law, inheritance, body claim, marriage, office, religion, and household
  belonging may treat the character as dead, living, property, dangerous, or an
  unresolved case;
- defeat may risk imprisonment, dismemberment, separation of initiative, or
  erosion of memory rather than ordinary death; and
- successor options may narrow, transform, or remain unavailable while the
  Revenant persists.

The transformation should preserve recognizable practice identity. It can open
a Revenant advancement branch and alter existing techniques rather than replace
the entire learned character with one generic undead kit.

## Lich distinction

A Revenant persists in its own dead body. A lich, if Maldirin ultimately uses the
term, has made initiative durable beyond that body's destruction through an
external work, distributed existence, replaceable body, or another greater
achievement. Lichdom belongs closer to campaign-scale ambition, catastrophe, or
ending space than a normal unlock.

Different cultures may use either word inaccurately. The design distinction
prevents every self-willed dead practitioner from inheriting the same powers.

## Proof requirements

Before adding a fifth starting seat:

1. Run a stripped martial encounter in which suspending and recalling functions
   changes at least three decisions beyond damage coefficients.
2. Carry one wound into treatment and prove that obscured pain or deferred
   circulation creates fair information rather than surprise punishment.
3. Fight one ordinary living opponent and one incorporeal threat without giving
   the class unrelated spell packages.
4. Test the same martial formation on two other practices to prove that warrior
   training and mortality practice are separate layers.
5. Paper-prototype one failed and one successful Revenant attempt across
   testament continuity, including the successor state after failure.
6. Reject the candidate if its optimal loop is to remain permanently pale, if
   its wounds are merely mana, or if it is simply the best melee class plus
   necromantic flavor.
