# World foundation and current-testament canon

This document separates established setting constraints from local facts and
working proposals. The opening testament can establish that a particular
county, road, or official exists without making it a universal institution.
New content must not promote implementation vocabulary into world canon.

## Canon status

- **Inherited:** Maldirin is the broader setting, probably the world; the
  implemented Medium, Bonebinder, and Renderer began as prototype methods.
- **User-established:** magic is uncommon and specialized; necromancers are a
  rare subset of practitioners; non-necromantic magic belongs to Maldirin's
  native order; ordinary people and mundane work remain central; communities
  differ in their treatment of necromancy; practitioners commonly protect a
  true name with a use-name. Magic follows the source, method, and consequence
  contract in [Magic](magic-and-environment.md); environmental dependence is
  one possible property of an art, not Maldirin's universal magical grammar.
- **Established primordial foundation:** the First knew itself in surprise and
  answered existence with gratitude. Existence was good, so the First gave
  being onward as lives genuinely their own. See
  [Cosmogenesis](cosmogenesis.md). The later formation of the Vast, Gods,
  worlds, and Maldirin remains open.
- **Provisional practice concepts:** Medium, Bonebinder, Renderer, and Summoner
  presently own the clearest distinct loops. A mortality practice centered on
  suspending and recalling the practitioner's own vital functions is a serious
  but unproved fifth-seat candidate. Their names, mechanics, traditions, and
  membership in a launch roster remain subject to review under the corrected
  magic model. Grave Warden/Cairnwright and Ferryman are candidates, offices, or
  regional paths rather than inherited world facts. See
  [the reassessment ledger](worldbuilding-reassessment.md).
- **Mechanical proving ground:** the Black Mile, Saint Orra, North Vey, the
  hollow tollhouse, county workers, and reeves currently exercise the game
  systems. None is a commitment to Maldirin’s canonical geography or history.
- **Working proposal:** the metaphysics, institutions, laws, currencies, and
  social structures below remain adjustable until Maldirin and its central
  conflict are deliberately established.

## Provisional cosmological foundation

The plurality of orders below remains the strongest working cosmological frame.
It is not tied to one central-conflict candidate. **Dominion on the March** was
an important developmental synthesis, but Nymorai and the Marching Country are
no longer the presumed campaign foundation. They remain shelved material beside
the Siege Sovereign, Moving Shade, Interdict, and other proof candidates until a
central conflict survives the new direct-play and world-coherence tests.

The root of cosmogenesis is established: the First's surprise and gratitude,
the goodness of existence, and the First's gift of genuine otherness. The
subsequent cosmogenesis is not settled. The current working model allows a
plurality of possible orders rather than one primordial list of substances.
Maldirin belongs to a broadly coherent, locally settled reality. “The Vast” is
an umbrella used for foreign orders, their intersections, and existence that has
not settled on Maldirin's terms; it is not outer space and need not be internally
uniform or chaotic. Other worlds may share Maldirin's broad order, sustain their
own coherent settlements, or meet it only at translated boundaries. Maldirin
became historically important through crossings, conquests, and inherited
works, not because it is necessarily the metaphysical center of creation.

Stable reality is not universal cosmic justice. Durable continuities are kept
by material regularity and by the accumulated work of gods, peoples, places,
and customs. Gods have many possible origins—enlarged ancestors, awakened
places or languages, land and household powers, refugee divinities, foreign
persons, inherited offices, and other enduring agencies. How this plurality
descends from or relates to the First's gift remains open.

Magic is one uncommon way of acting within Maldirin's native causality, not
merely foreign weirdness leaking into mundane scenes. Every art has a source,
method, consequences, limits, and counters, but they do not share one
metaphysical currency or mandatory environmental grammar. Trained or altered
capacity can travel with a practitioner. Materials, targets, other persons,
artifacts, relationships, time, and place matter only as each capability gives
them reason to matter. The same authored limits bind NPCs and the player.

A living person observably has body, breath, and initiative. What survives
death, what reaches shelter, and whether any account is complete remain disputed
inside the world. Necromancy can introduce autonomous initiative into prepared
bodies, objects, and relationships. It is unusual, dangerous, and relevant to
the central conflict without being the chosen or uniquely necessary answer to
it. Any starting practices that survive prototyping should express materially
different relationships with embodiment, agency, remains, territory, and
passage rather than partitioning one generic spell list.

Some ancient Maldirin powers invaded and harvested the Vast. The present age may
inherit captive powers, abandoned colonies, ruined roads, displaced peoples,
retaliation, and precursor rivals, but no single empire performed every ancient
act and no present conflict automatically descends from that history. Present
responsibility follows present action rather than ancestry. Dormant powers,
precursor pockets, and old patrons can re-emerge independently, offering,
coercing, levying, annexing, or exploiting people destabilized by current
change. Each requires specific provenance whether or not anyone in play knows
it.

Shelved central-conflict material remains available without becoming background
law: Nymorai's contestable continuities; bodily answerability and truthful
repair; household guest-right and breach forensics; refugee gods and culturally
intact agents; ancient tombs under obsolete covenants; the Good Lady's remote
cosmology; the Siege Sovereign's embodied conquest; the Moving Shade's chosen
imperial protection; and the Interdict's coercive monopoly. Do not strip a
candidate for atmospheric parts until its distinctive causal structure becomes
indistinguishable from the setting in general.

Guardrails for development:

- Keep ordinary reality stable outside bounded supernatural incursions.
- Give supernatural action legible causes, limits, and counters; do not turn
  every encounter into a bespoke metaphysical riddle or force unrelated arts
  through one favored grammar.
- Never equate age with truth, legitimacy, or moral right.
- Let interior politics, travel, trade, kinship, labor, crime, and war support
  campaigns that do not concern Nymorai, the Vast, or necromancy.
- Give every supernatural premise lived customs and material consequences
  before adding more cosmological vocabulary.
- Develop three grounded regions and at least one non-Nymorai,
  non-necromancy-driven campaign as the next test before promoting this
  foundation to established canon.

## Confirmed foundations

The normative grounding for native and foreign magic and for gods and Gods
lives in [Magic: sources, methods, and consequences](magic-and-environment.md).
Its rules are established independently of the still-open later cosmogenesis.

- Magic is specialized labor. Aptitude is uncommon, useful training is narrow, and necromancers are a minority among practitioners. A beginning necromancer is inexperienced by the standards of the practice and still exceptional to nearly everyone they meet.
- Non-necromantic magic exists and follows Maldirin's native causal order.
  Practitioners are usually encountered with the bodily capacities, knowledge,
  tools, preparations, patrons, collaborators, and circumstances that make
  their specialization credible. A genuine limitation is real for NPCs as well
  as the player.
- Trained and altered practitioners commonly carry meaningful agency with them.
  A capability may make a condition essential, substitutable, amplifying,
  shaping, resisting, or irrelevant. Hard environmental gates are uncommon and
  specific; they are not imposed indiscriminately on supernatural action.
- Necromancy does not define ordinary life. Work, injury, scarcity, authority,
  family, and local custom remain consequential without supernatural causes.
- Communities and individuals judge necromancy differently. The same act may
  be requested, tolerated, feared, or punished according to people, place,
  knowledge, and circumstance.
- Necromancers commonly protect a true name with a use-name.
- Witness and evidence are different. A hidden act still happened. A witnessed act may be misunderstood. Evidence can travel without a witness, and a witness can choose silence. Factions should react only to what reaches them.
- A region makes some specialists, patrons, materials, institutions, and
  techniques plausible; it does not grant every locality the same capabilities.
  Site, route, catchment, access, preparation, degradation, opportunity, and
  cost remain distinct when an authored act depends upon them. They must not be
  invented merely to make every practitioner environmentally situated.

## Provisional present-day campaign frame

These decisions are the current shared foundation for central-conflict and
regional design. They remain broad enough to be revised when grounded content
proves them inadequate.

- Maldirin is a multi-continental world. The first major campaign begins in a
  primary contiguous continental theater and may expand beyond it later.
  Ordinary travel and news take real time. Rare magic, inherited works, and
  exceptional powers may violate those limits, but doing so is noteworthy.
- The theater has a layered, asymmetric political order. Several old and
  substantially different regional orders coexist; none rules the whole. It
  includes large sclerotic powers, some ambitious or expansionist states, small
  non-expansionist local sovereignties made unusually secure by a god, nonhuman
  civilization, environment, or inherited work, and broad interstitial country
  where authority remains contested or negotiable. Neither conquest nor recent
  collapse defines the whole age.
- Maldirin's great decline is ancient. Ordinary human generations have no living
  cultural memory of what preceded it. Current civilizations are real societies
  with long histories of their own rather than provisional camps beneath ruins.
  A player may nevertheless decide that the long decline is a deeper problem
  than the campaign's advertised crisis.
- The present age is one of uneven renewal: greater movement, ambition, contact,
  exploitation, construction, reclamation, and discovery produced by many
  independent actors. It is not a universal renaissance, a single force's work,
  or uniformly beneficial. Some places remain stagnant, insulated, or continue
  to degrade.
- Maldirin has many futures and no common future. People and powers pursue aims
  shaped by their histories, attachments, dependencies, injuries, opportunities,
  and time horizons. Many seek to leave something worth inheriting, but disagree
  about its substance, heirs, and acceptable cost. Others seek release from
  inheritance, immediate survival, mastery, conquest, revelation, pleasure, or
  ends that admit no successor. Motivation must follow provenance rather than
  being assigned as procedural color.
- Gods and cosmological Gods both exist. Their agency is inherent rather than
  borrowed from a universal network of sites or worship. A god may be locally
  sovereign or widely traveling; a God may act across lands, ages, worlds,
  species, or orders of reality. Their limits remain individual and
  commensurate—rivals, incompatible orders, divided attention, self-binding,
  internal contradiction, ancient defeat, or the consequences of acting at full
  magnitude—not routine absence of a shrine or aspect. Supernatural power varies
  independently in intensity, reach, portability, reliability, autonomy,
  persistence, and discretion.
- A central conflict requires layered adversarial presence. The player must be
  able to recognize, anticipate, influence, and oppose at least one recurring
  will: the mover, an aspect, a principal agent, or an actor exploiting an
  impersonal force. The ultimate source may remain remote or partly unknowable;
  all accountable agency may not remain hidden behind phenomena.
- When the mover is a God, ordinary defenses should be manifestly insufficient.
  Play concerns surviving, understanding, redirecting, opposing agents,
  frustrating an intervention, exploiting a true vulnerability, or changing
  what that God can accomplish—not reducing its totality to a boss with a missing
  local power source.
- The opening chapter has a soft experiential target rather than a plot clock.
  In an ordinary first passage, the player should have opportunities to use
  their practice as a genuine way of acting, encounter consequential disturbance
  with credible signs of agency, gain enough understanding to choose a response,
  pass through meaningful route divergence, and leave a bounded consequence the
  world can retain. Sequence and timing vary with play style, travel, ambition,
  refusal, failure, and successor circumstance. Declining the central conflict
  redirects play; it does not start a punitive timer whose purpose is to herd the
  player back to the plot.

## Proving-ground locality: North Vey

The mechanical testament currently takes place north of Vey, along a wet county
margin of tenant fields, exhausted limestone pits, and villages built beyond
older graves. The Black Mile is not a separate otherworld. It is one worked
road with toll markers, burial ditches, abandoned county property, and a
history that has become physically unsafe. This does not establish “burial
road” as a recognized world category.

The remaining details in this section explain the current demo’s internal
logic. They are material to test against, not established Maldirin lore.

County reeves keep the roads, carry warrants, escort tax and burial traffic, and enforce a magistrate’s orders farther than the magistrate will travel. Their authority is real but thin. A patrol may be days from reinforcement. A corrupt reeve can make a private toll look official until someone survives it.

Bodies remain under household claim until burial and under county protection while being carried. Grave goods may pass to shrines, licensed reliquary brokers, or those who lawfully put down a dangerous dead. Opening a recent body for occult materials without leave is both theft and desecration. Practical need may change how a witness judges the act; it does not erase the act.

## Working metaphysics from the proving ground

A name is the part of a person most easily retained without flesh. Funeral speech, ledgers, debt rolls, family use, and ward-names all leave different holds on it. This is why Saint Orra can keep names without keeping whole souls, and why freeing them does not guarantee that each will depart cleanly.

Necromancers commonly work under a use-name and keep a true name out of ordinary speech and record. A use-name is not worthless camouflage: offering it freely lets the living vouch for, summon, accuse, or remember the practitioner, and lets lesser dead address someone without being given the deepest hold on them. Giving it can establish trust. It can also put a stable identity into a burial book, witness statement, or reeve post.

Names, debts, vows, and ledgers matter here, but the proving ground does not
establish them as the universal center of Maldirin's necromancy. They may belong
to particular regions, institutions, shared techniques, or later specializations.

Physical remains preserve condition and history. Bone, fat, blood salts, grave wax, and the residue left by occult violence can become reagents. Quality depends on freshness, contamination, the manner of death, and the practitioner’s skill. Rendering is not a guaranteed conversion of corpse into loot; it is difficult work performed on material that communities still regard as a person.

Incorporeal dead do not wear ordinary armor. Their resistance comes from incomplete materiality, names, vows, wards, or the form holding them together. This gives physical and occult damage distinct places in the world rather than treating them as interchangeable colors.

## Proving-ground and candidate practices in society

These entries describe consequences worth preserving while the roster is under
review. Medium, Bonebinder, Renderer, and Summoner are strong provisional
concepts. The mortality practice is a serious proof candidate rather than an
established category. Grave Warden and Ferryman language may instead name
regional offices, traditions, or advanced paths. None is a universal social
category recognized unchanged across Maldirin.

### Mediums

Mediums cross a boundary in their own bodies. Families may seek one for testimony, last words, identification, or the settlement of a disputed death. The same household may fear possession, false testimony, and whatever remains in the medium after the dead has spoken.

### Bonebinders

Bonebinders make command, identity, and bodily custody impossible to ignore.
Their servant may be welcomed as an ancestor, licensed as dangerous labor,
refused at a gate, or recognized as stolen kin. The provenance of the body and
the terms under which it works matter as much as the fact that it walks.

Ditch-Hand is the authored servant of the proving-ground testament, not the
default identity of every Bonebinder's dead. A full campaign creates a servant
identity deterministically from the persisted run seed, its body and provenance,
and the culture that named it. The generated identity is then saved as fact;
loading a testament never rolls or reconstructs another name.

### Renderers

Renderers work closest to trades people recognize—apothecary, rendering,
preservation, surgery—and cross the clearest material taboos. Provenance matters.
Animal remains, surrendered grave goods, hostile manifestations, executed
bodies, and a neighbor cut open beside the road do not carry the same social
meaning.

Rendering should use seeded checks. Better training can increase the chance of a
usable yield, the quantity recovered, and access to rarer materials. Place,
time, concealment, Perception, and Survival can separately determine whether the
work is witnessed or leaves discoverable evidence.

### Summoners

Summoners make a formation of external dead visible as a social presence. A few
recognizable shades, a collective of impressions, and a host coerced from local
dead do not carry the same claims or fears. Communities may distinguish honored
retainers from anonymous levies, forbid either inside walls, or lack any settled
custom because no living resident has met such a practitioner.

### Martial necromancers and mortality practitioners

`Martial necromancer` most often describes training, office, or formation rather
than one practice. The same order may teach a Medium, Bonebinder, Renderer, or
Summoner to fight in armor, treat battlefield wounds, hold a breach, and oppose
hostile manifestations. Its history and equipment belong to the society that
formed it; warrior training does not imply one Maldirin-wide class.

A rarer candidate practice turns upon the necromancer's own vital state. Such a
practitioner may arrest breath, circulation, sensation, pain, warmth, or another
living function and fight while its return remains uncertain. One tradition may
regard complete return as its proof; another may accept lasting suspension and
bodily change. This remains provisional until direct play proves the distinction.

### Revenants and liches

A Revenant is a person whose own initiative persists in their own dead body.
There is no routine recipe and no assumption that every necromancer can become
one. A character may discover a practice-specific route involving knowledge,
preparation, continuance, controlled death, irreversible price, and genuine
failure. The transformation preserves and changes the original practice rather
than replacing it with one universal undead kit.

A lich, if a culture uses the word accurately, has made initiative durable
beyond one body's destruction through an external work, distributed existence,
replaceable embodiment, or another greater achievement. Either term may be used
loosely in-world. The design distinction keeps an exceptional Revenant from
automatically inheriting campaign-scale immortality.

### Grave Wardens

`Grave Warden` is currently more secure as an office or lineage than as a
universal practice. Such wardens may maintain graves, threatened bodies,
mortuary fittings, or local covenants. One community may treat a Warden's work
as lawful shelter while another sees an outsider enforcing obsolete claims.
Preserving an inherited arrangement is not automatically just.

### Ferrymen

Where a culture possesses a real mode of dead passage, `Ferryman` may name an
office or tradition that conducts, retrieves, returns, or denies it. Its tools
may be recognized as signs of refuge and safe conduct or feared as notice that a
departure is imminent. Another region may recognize neither the passage nor the
title. This remains a candidate, not evidence of one Maldirin-wide afterlife.

## Powers and attitudes

### County reeves

Reeves may tolerate wardcraft, the quieting of a dangerous dead, examination ordered by law, and relic work with clear ownership. They prosecute public occult violence, coerced testimony, body theft, and unauthorized raising. Individual reeves still differ: fear, corruption, prior loss, and orders from above matter.

### County burial crews

Gravediggers, corpse drivers, bell keepers, and ditch crews possess practical knowledge without occult mastery. They know when specialist help is necessary and when a practitioner has left ordinary workers to contain the result. Assistance with burial earns a different memory than damage to a named body.

### Reliquary brokers

Brokers turn dangerous remains into licensed trade. They value stability and provenance because a useful relic becomes contraband when a household identifies the bone. They may like a practitioner’s goods and dislike the attention following them.

### North-road households

There is no universal village opinion of necromancy. Need, kinship, local saints, religious custom, and remembered harm matter more than a setting-wide alignment. Very few people have met a necromancer. Most will judge the first one by what happens to the bodies, names, and living people nearby.

## State carried by the campaign

Campaign persistence across characters, successor relationships, Chronicle
separation, irreversible retirement, and persistent holdings follow the
normative [campaign and testament continuity contract](testament-continuity.md).
The world model below remains authoritative campaign history rather than a
character-owned memory that resets with a new body.

The game distinguishes:

- **facts**: what actually happened;
- **player knowledge**: facts the character can name and the Journal may safely expose;
- **beliefs**: what a person or faction accepts, at what confidence, and which
  evidence supports it;
- **evidence**: a witness account, body, object, record, or occult trace that
  can be held and moved without changing the fact it concerns;
- **claims and provenance**: who asserts ownership, custody, authority, or
  identity over a subject, where it came from, and how custody changed;
- **relationships**: membership, employment, kinship, custody, and obligation,
  independently of approval;
- **standing**: how a faction or individual currently regards the character;
- **pressure**: scoped active conditions with stakeholders and source facts,
  such as escaped names gaining distance or the county taking notice.

These are persisted separately. The Journal reads player knowledge, never the
complete fact ledger or another actor’s beliefs. A secret rendering records the
deed and is known to the character, but does not make the reeves omniscient.
Evidence may exist without reaching them. A witness can report it, give the
reeves a credible belief, worsen county standing, and raise notice. That notice
can change route eligibility and turn a formerly random patrol into a direct
consequence. The older faction-knowledge and numeric-pressure fields remain
save-compatible projections while authored content migrates to the structured
records.

Journal world entries use explicit player-safe disclosures rather than
serializing arbitrary world records. Known people and parties, relationships,
evidence, claims, current custody, and qualitative active concerns each require
an authored disclosure plus a fact the character knows. Confidence levels,
evidence strength, standing, pressure intensity, unknown custody history, and
other actors’ beliefs remain outside that read model.

The disputed blackcloth bundle on the North Road is the first noncombat scene
to exercise the full separation. Eda Pell and Oren Vask begin with different
grounds for judging the bundle’s origin, can watch the same optional
examination, and revise their beliefs differently. The player may then leave
the sealed bundle under burial custody, release it to the broker, or decline to
settle the dispute. Facts, evidence, claims, custody, relationships, and local
standing change independently; no global moral verdict is recorded.

New social content should avoid a single global morality score. Record the act, its witnesses, its evidence, and the affected relationship. Different people can then respond for different reasons.

The production actor catalog gives every proving-ground faction and recurring
person an explicit attitude toward each committed practice. These attitudes
name a posture and concerns such as consent, body claim, provenance, custody,
continuity, or safe passage. They describe this locality’s actors, not a
Maldirin-wide rule. A burial crew may seek a Grave Warden and refuse a
Bonebinder, while a household’s reaction to either remains variable according
to kinship and circumstance.

## Voice

The prose is dry, dark, and foreboding. It should describe material facts with enough restraint that the implication arrives before the explanation.

- Prefer rain entering an opened vessel to a narrator making a joke about corpse work.
- Prefer a reeve naming the cuts in a body to a generic “evil reputation” notification.
- Let institutions, tools, wounds, and customs carry dread.
- Avoid clever asides, cute reversals, punchlines, mock-bureaucratic quips, and metaphors that congratulate themselves.
- Keep occasional lyricism, but make it concrete and consequential.
- Do not imitate another game’s narrator, cadence, vocabulary, or sentence architecture.
