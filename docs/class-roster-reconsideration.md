# Player-practice roster reconsideration

> **Follow-up:** [Practice and tradition ideation pass](class-and-tradition-ideation.md)
> restores Summoner as a distinct serious candidate and moves the Anatomist's
> strongest material into opposing Bonebinder traditions. This earlier memo is
> retained for its candidate criticism and proof criteria, not as the current
> roster recommendation. It also predates the corrected magic contract; any
> argument that treats environmental situatedness as a roster-wide identity is
> historical rather than governing guidance. The later
> [martial-necromancy and Revenancy contract](martial-necromancy-and-revenancy.md)
> separates cross-practice warrior training, a mortality-practice candidate,
> and earned Revenancy; its conclusions supersede this memo's Anatomist and
> self-transformation ranking.

Status: **provisional ideation memo**. Nothing here promotes a practice, name,
mechanic, or cosmological account to canon. In particular, the five-practice
roster should no longer be cited as an inherited world constraint until its two
uncertain seats survive direct-play proofs.

## Recommendation in brief

Treat **Medium, Bonebinder, and Renderer** as the strongest stable conceptual
core, while continuing to regard their present abilities and balance as
prototype implementations. Do not yet commit **Grave Warden** or **Ferryman** as
starting practices.

The clearest near-term roster is four practices: rebuild the useful material
parts of Grave Warden as a battlefield-shaping **Cairnwright** concept, then
prove it beside the stable three. Keep *grave warden* available as the name of a
regional office, tradition, or Cairnwright specialization. Treat Ferryman as a
possible culture-specific tradition or later advanced practice until Maldirin
actually establishes more than one credible account of dead passage.

If a fifth starting practice is valuable, an anatomy-centered **Anatomist** is
currently a stronger direct-play candidate than Ferryman. It would modify and
risk the necromancer's living body through consequential grafts rather than
govern an assumed universal road for the dead.

## Strongest stable core

“Stable” below means worth carrying into the next proof, not immutable.

### Medium — keep the identity; replace the prototype spell kit

The Medium owns a clear, intimate act: admitting dead initiative into a living
body. That remains distinctive with all flavor text removed. It naturally
supports character attachment because guests are recurring persons with wants,
knowledge, relationships, recognizable movement, and terms.

Its direct-play loop should be **admit, embody, overdraw, and dismiss**:

- enter an encounter with one prepared invitation and perhaps one dangerous
  unprepared opening;
- let a guest alter locomotion, weapon handling, defense, perception, and one
  or two active techniques rather than merely adding occult damage;
- expose strain through concrete body parts, impaired control, and contested
  action—not routine Health payment for ordinary output; and
- recover Spirit or another bounded advantage by completing or honestly ending
  a guest's passage, never by farming low-risk damage.

The existing Spirit Lash and Borrowed Might are serviceable test actions but do
not yet prove the practice. The proof must make switching or retaining a guest
more important than selecting the largest coefficient. Dead testimony remains
partial and interested; it is not the Medium's universal scene-solver.

### Bonebinder — keep and deepen

Bonebinder is the strongest field-readable fantasy in the roster: the player
travels with a corporeal dead servant whose body, equipment, damage, history,
and social presence persist. It reuses the anatomy and equipment systems while
making provenance visible instead of relegating it to journal text.

Its direct-play loop should be **position, direct, support, and repair**:

- a standing order persists until changed;
- choosing or revising an ordinary direction should not consume the
  necromancer's entire action;
- exceptional exertions, forced contradictions, emergency interposition, and
  complex body memories may consume Spirit, tempo, or trust;
- player and servant act as a coordinated pair, not as one actor plus passive
  damage and not as two unrelated full characters; and
- body loss changes available movement and techniques, while safe repair uses
  shared anatomy and real materials.

Later branches can emphasize one carefully maintained partner, replaceable
composite construction, or short-lived impressions seated in encountered
remains. The latter can provide some of the immediate corpse-play associated
with action necromancers without turning every corpse into a permanent party
member.

### Renderer — keep the name and make preparation physical

Renderer owns death-altered material. It supports ranged attacks, coatings,
field hazards, forensic work, treatment, trade, risk of contamination, and
uneasy relations with both ordinary craftspeople and authorities. The name is
specific without pretending that all magic is “thaumaturgy.”

Its direct-play loop should be **prepare, expose, react, and reclaim**:

- carry a small visible rack of prepared applications rather than a warehouse
  of microscopic ingredients;
- apply substances to weapons, bodies, ground, fires, water, armor, and wounds;
- combine two known properties into a readable reaction when positioning or
  target anatomy makes it worthwhile; and
- replenish through meaningful remains, services, purchases, and batch work,
  not mandatory clicks on every corpse.

Renderer must not quietly become the best doctor, crafter, merchant, forensic
expert, and damage dealer. Recipes should create commitments and matchup
advantages. Ordinary Medicine, skilled fabrication, and trade remain useful
without necromancy.

## Uncertain candidates

### Grave Warden — useful world role, weak present practice ontology

The attractive parts are tangible: defended graves, mortuary hardware,
interposition, stabilizing a threatened body, preventing an incorporeal thing
from passing a marked boundary, and preserving evidence. “Grave Warden” also
sounds like a credible office that different societies might license, fear, or
assign.

The weak part is what the current practice claims to own. **Continuity and
custody are broad abstractions**, not an immediately playable substance or act.
They can expand until every promise, wall, identity, body, polity, and status
becomes Warden material. Current seals attached to individual enemies also do
not behave like visible boundaries. “Answer the Breach” rewards a failure the
player arranged, invites the setting's recurring occult-reciprocity prose, and
can turn minor enemies into a safe Ward-and-Spirit engine.

The strongest salvage is a material recast provisionally called
**Cairnwright**:

- carry grave stakes, cord, nails, ash, marked stones, or prepared fittings;
- place a small number of physical anchors on traversable ground;
- join anchors into visible lines that reveal, halt, divert, or punish an
  appropriate crossing;
- raise temporary cover or close a corpse against hostile use;
- deliberately collapse or pull a boundary for an active payoff; and
- let living enemies cut, pull, go around, or seize its anchors.

This is battlefield geometry rather than free defense. A burial ground,
prepared masonry, or local mortuary authority may amplify particular workings;
the portable kit still gives the practice a bounded answer on an ordinary road.
“Grave Warden” can then be a Veyward institution or Cairnwright tradition whose
members emphasize custody and refuge. If the spatial proof is not enjoyable,
the whole candidate should be demoted rather than rescued by more lore.

### Ferryman — evocative title, unearned universal premise

Escort, retrieval, exile, merciful endings, and preventing a dangerous return
are fertile story material. Ferryman also offers a potentially valuable tempo
role: act at defeat transitions, extract an ally, or deny an enemy's second
state.

But the practice currently assumes that dead initiative has routes and
thresholds coherent enough to form a portable profession across Maldirin. The
world has deliberately left shelters, persistence after death, and divine
accounts plural and disputed. Generic “passage” powers can become teleportation
with funeral vocabulary, while guaranteed usefulness against mundane bandits
pushes the design toward arbitrary marks and status transfers.

Ferryman is presently stronger as one of these:

1. a regional tradition practiced by Mediums, Cairnwrights, or others where a
   culture has established a particular funerary road;
2. an advanced practice learned after the player encounters and chooses to
   maintain a real mode of passage; or
3. a future starting practice only after it works in an ordinary fight, a
   manifestation encounter, and two regions with incompatible accounts of the
   dead without declaring one account false by game-system fiat.

Its best ideas should not be discarded. Clean defeat transitions, retrieval,
refuge, and the prevention of recurrence can live as shared techniques,
traditions, equipment, or authored divine relationships while the seat remains
open.

### Anatomist — strongest replacement candidate, still expensive

An Anatomist treats death as a source of compatible structures and makes the
living necromancer's own body the persistent build. This is not a Renderer
preparing substances or a Bonebinder maintaining another body.

Its loop is **select, graft, exert, and recover**:

- install only a few consequential grafts at safe treatment;
- let anatomy and equipment visibly change the field sprite and portrait;
- activate a graft for movement, reach, perception, defense, or violence;
- accept fatigue, rejection, changed wound care, social recognition, and loss
  of ordinary equipment compatibility; and
- acquire exceptional structures from authored creatures or difficult trades,
  not routine human dismemberment chores.

This gives direct melee and body-build play a natural home. Its risks are high
presentation cost, overlap with Renderer acquisition and Medium bodily danger,
and the possibility of reducing unusual beings to upgrade parts. It is a proof
candidate, not a recommendation to canonize self-grafting throughout Maldirin.

## Alternative roster configurations

### Configuration A — recommended four-practice launch proof

1. Medium
2. Bonebinder
3. Renderer
4. Cairnwright, with Grave Warden as one tradition or office

This gives embodiment, companion command, material preparation, and spatial
control. All four produce visible action in the direct-play field and have
different recovery and social problems. Ferryman remains available for later
worldbuilding without making the launch roster carry unresolved cosmology.

**Risk:** four practices may feel conservative, and Cairnwright can overlap
Renderer traps or Bonebinder corpse control unless it owns ground geometry and
neither of those does.

### Configuration B — five-practice body-and-field roster

1. Medium
2. Bonebinder
3. Renderer
4. Cairnwright
5. Anatomist

This is the strongest current five-seat action-RPG configuration. It adds a
personal melee/body-build fantasy rather than another abstract authority. The
field reads clearly: possessed living actor, maintained servant, prepared
materials, physical boundaries, and altered player anatomy.

**Risk:** this is the most expensive visual and anatomical configuration. It
also needs firm ethical and economic handling so harvesting does not become the
default reward loop for both Renderer and Anatomist.

### Configuration C — preserve the current names, demand harder proofs

1. Medium
2. Bonebinder
3. Renderer
4. rebuilt Grave Warden
5. rebuilt Ferryman

Grave Warden adopts the Cairnwright's physical anchor play while retaining the
current name. Ferryman operates only through prepared remains, funeral
implements, known manifestations, and observed defeat transitions—never a
generic movement spell. Each must remain useful when no grave, spirit, or death
has yet appeared.

**Risk:** this saves nomenclature but not necessarily design. Warden can still
inflate “continuity” into universal jurisdiction, and Ferryman mechanics can
silently canonize one afterlife because the roster needs buttons in every
encounter.

### Configuration D — deliberately open fourth and fifth seats

Launch proofs only for Medium, Bonebinder, and Renderer. Treat Grave Warden,
Cairnwright, Ferryman, Anatomist, and future candidates as competitive modules,
not promised classes. Promote a candidate only when its stripped mechanical
loop is more fun than adding its best techniques as an advancement or regional
tradition to the stable three.

**Risk:** three choices may undersell the intended breadth, and postponing a
field-control practice could bias encounter construction around the existing
classes. The benefit is avoiding expensive canon and save commitments made to
fill a symmetrical roster.

## Mechanical and world-fit comparison

| Candidate | What the player repeatedly manipulates | Direct-play strength | Credible ordinary-world work | Principal risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Medium | guest identity and control of their own body | adaptable stance/technique changes | testimony, recognition, negotiated hosting | guests become spell loadouts or universal witnesses |
| Bonebinder | one persistent corporeal servant and its orders | two-body positioning, interception, equipment, repair | labor, custody, dangerous recovery | command tax makes the player spectate; provenance becomes paperwork |
| Renderer | limited prepared applications and material reactions | coatings, projectiles, hazards, countermeasures | forensics, treatment, rendering, specialist trade | inventory chores and mastery of too many mundane skills |
| Cairnwright / rebuilt Warden | portable anchors and bounded ground | walls, lines, denial, exposure, sacrificial fields | grave security, dangerous-site containment, evidence protection | defensive stalling or abstract “continuity” law |
| Ferryman | prepared passages and observed death transitions | extraction, finishing, recurrence denial, tempo | funeral escort and recovery where culturally real | cosmology by convenience; funeral-flavored teleportation |
| Anatomist | a few installed living-body grafts | melee adaptation, movement, body-risk builds | rare surgery and anatomical expertise | asset/anatomy cost; beings become loot containers |

The world should classify practitioners from observed work and local history,
not from a universal five-color taxonomy. A Cairnwright can be licensed as a
grave warden in one polity and treated as a trespassing ward-setter in another.
A Ferryman tradition may be revered beside one river and meaningless two
regions away. Practice definitions supply mechanics; traditions, offices,
backgrounds, and laws supply situated identity.

## Overlap and design guardrails

- **Medium versus Anatomist:** both use the living self, but Medium changes
  initiative and control while Anatomist changes material capability and wound
  consequences. Do not give both generic body-overdraw as their whole economy.
- **Bonebinder versus Cairnwright:** Bonebinder owns an actor; Cairnwright owns
  ground and fittings. A corpse closed by a cairn may deny everyone, including
  the Bonebinder, but does not become the Cairnwright's servant.
- **Renderer versus Anatomist:** Renderer creates portable applications;
  Anatomist makes slow, persistent body commitments. Shared source materials
  should create competing uses, not duplicated harvesting menus.
- **Cairnwright versus Ferryman:** the first candidate uses prepared fittings to
  constrain specific bodies or manifestations; the second changes a culturally
  and metaphysically real mode of dead passage. If a single effect can be
  assigned to either by changing its noun, one of the seats is not ready.
- No practice owns permission, contracts, names, debts, truth, or environmental
  conditions in general. Those concepts remain available to people,
  institutions, mundane skills, items, traditions, and other magic.
- Every starting practice needs useful, legible actions before a corpse falls,
  in a location without its favored infrastructure, and against an ordinary
  living opponent.
- Signature state should deepen choices, not add a private chore screen. The
  direct-play field, body/equipment view, and brief safe preparation should
  carry most interaction.

## Recommended next proof tests

1. **Cairnwright A/B proof.** Run the same mixed fight with the existing
   target-attached Grave Warden seals and with two or three physically placed
   anchors. Strip the prose. Measure movement decisions, useful enemy
   counterplay, stalled rounds, damage avoided, and whether the player can
   predict the field. Promote neither version by default.
2. **Ferryman falsification proof.** Test one ordinary roadside attack with no
   dead present, one incorporeal recurrence, and one rescue across a culturally
   established funerary boundary. If its ordinary-fight kit reads as generic
   teleport/status magic or the other scenes require one universal dead road,
   demote it to tradition or advanced practice.
3. **Anatomist paper-and-sprite proof.** Offer three mutually exclusive grafts
   that visibly change silhouette, action reach, equipment compatibility, and
   treatment. Carry one wound through recovery. Reject the candidate if the
   choice is merely `+damage` or requires routine corpse harvesting to remain
   solvent.
4. **Stable-core direct-play gauntlet.** Give Medium, Bonebinder, and Renderer
   the same mundane bandit fight, armored corporeal monster, incorporeal threat,
   rescue under environmental pressure, and hostile settlement entrance. Each
   should expose a different repeatable loop without bespoke class-only scene
   exits.
5. **Tempo and solvency test.** Simulate eight-round weak and strong encounters.
   No practice should profit from indefinite defense, require self-harm for
   routine damage, lose its whole turn to routine companion direction, or arrive
   at every second fight without access to its identity.
6. **World portability test.** Describe how each candidate is taught, equipped,
   employed, and judged in two societies that disagree about bodies and the
   dead. If the mechanic only makes sense under one institution's vocabulary,
   classify it as a tradition rather than a universal starting practice.

The next roster decision should follow these proofs. There is no design virtue
in committing to five seats before five direct-play identities have earned
them.
