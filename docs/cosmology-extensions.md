# Cosmology extensions: compatible directions

Status: design proposals. This document does not establish new canon.

This document records extensions originally developed around **Dominion on the
March**. Dominion is no longer the presumed central conflict, so its Nymorai-
specific sections are candidate material rather than a foundation to extend.
The document still separates inherited constraints from recommendations worth
testing and modules that can remain remote, regional, or false within the
setting. An extension becomes canon only after it survives grounded regions,
ordinary lives, and a campaign in which necromancy is one useful answer rather
than the cosmological master key.

## Invariant constraints

The following are not opened by this proposal:

- Maldirin is a fallen, materially dangerous setting in which ordinary reality
  remains dependable outside bounded supernatural incursions.
- Magical arts have distinct sources, methods, consequences, costs, affinities,
  limits, and failures. Relationships are one possible basis; there is no
  universal occult currency or environmental grammar disguised by different
  spell names.
- Necromancers are uncommon specialists. Current practice names are provisional
  mechanical hypotheses, not alignments, exhaustive schools, or settled facts
  of cosmology.
- Necromancy may become important to a central conflict, but soldiers, river
  workers, priests, engineers, diplomats, ordinary households, and other
  practitioners must remain capable of acting upon the same conflict.
- Nymorai's promise of liberation is real, and the Marching Country's evil is
  real. Perpetual contest destroys the refuge required by the weak and makes
  return unreliable. It is not a blank force waiting for the player to assign it
  a morality.
- Ancient origin does not confer truth, legitimacy, innocence, or ownership.
  Present responsibility follows present choices.
- Gods may have many origins. No single account of the dead, divinity, or the
  Vast is complete merely because a powerful institution teaches it.
- The primordial foundation is established in
  [Cosmogenesis](cosmogenesis.md): surprise, gratitude, the goodness of
  existence, and the First's gift of genuine otherness. Later plurality may be
  disputed; these roots are not reopened by a regional cosmology.
- Names, debts, ledgers, and grave law may be important locally without becoming
  the universal grammar of existence.

## Provisional foundation retained

The root of cosmogenesis is established; its later development remains open.
The current working model allows a plurality of coherent orders rather than
treating creation as one primordial list of incompletely separated concepts.
Maldirin belongs to a locally settled reality. “The Vast” is an umbrella for
foreign orders, their intersections, and existence that has not settled on
Maldirin's terms; individual Vast realities may be highly coherent and need not
resemble one another. Other worlds may share Maldirin's broad order or sustain
different local settlements. Durable continuities make ordinary life possible,
and gods, peoples, places, customs, material regularity, and repeated work can
defend them without proving them just. This later cosmogenesis is provisional;
the source, method, consequence, and capability-specific limits in
[the magic contract](magic-and-environment.md) are established without depending
on a final account of how orders and worlds formed. Environment is one possible
source, affinity, or obstacle, not the universal grammar of magic.

Ancient Maldirin powers entered the Vast, harvested powers and territories, and
left captive forces, broken colonies, and abandoned roads behind. Nymorai's
Marching Country now incorporates places by overturning the continuities that
hold them. It is a moving constitution rather than an advancing wall or a plague
of mindless converts.

The five recommendations below clarify how that foundation can become playable.
They are compatible with one another, but each should be tested independently
before all five are treated as one mandatory cosmological package.

## Strong recommendations

### 1. The Carrion Inheritance

The present age lives among the remains of dead supernatural sovereignties.
Some are literal carcasses. Others are extinct climates, abandoned divine
offices, severed roads, or Vast-born organisms whose surviving parts were made
into aqueducts, fortifications, medicines, dynastic regalia, and agricultural
works. Maldirin is not merely built over ruins; portions of ordinary prosperity
still depend on things the old powers killed, subdued, or left unable to finish
dying.

This should be called an inheritance rather than a universal historical age
until chronology and regional vocabulary are settled. The useful claim is
material: old violence remains inside functioning structures.

Human consequences:

- A town may depend on a dead river-power's ribbed spillway without knowing its
  origin. Closing it is not a clean moral act when fields will fail downstream.
- Guilds, temples, houses, and states can hold practical monopolies over ancient
  remains. Their authority may be useful, predatory, or both.
- Scholars can disagree about whether a structure is a corpse, a machine, a
  captive, or an ecology. The answer changes what repair, release, and ownership
  mean.
- Trade in old matter can be ordinary, sacred, restricted, or criminal according
  to provenance and present harm rather than one global taboo.

Gameplay consequences:

- Exploration can expose an old work through failure, maintenance, or competing
  claims rather than a lore monologue.
- Materials gain strategic alternatives: keep a fragment for equipment, sell it
  to a specialist with present demand, surrender it to restore a public work, or
  retain it as evidence of a larger danger.
- Other practices remain essential. Engineers can shore the structure, priests
  can renegotiate its cult, natural magicians can restore its ecology, and armies
  can seize it. Necromancers have distinctive access to dead initiative and
  remains, not sole authority.

Limit: not every bridge is a god's femur and not every useful object is secretly
alive. Most tools and public works remain human work made from ordinary matter.

### 2. Continuity has anchors, not a currency

Use a small relational grammar to make supernatural continuity legible without
turning every custom into a cosmic courtroom. A durable continuity usually has
several of the following anchors:

- **a seat**: a body, place, object, species, or material arrangement in which
  it is present;
- **a practice**: repeated labor, rite, habit, or natural recurrence that renews
  it;
- **recognition**: living or supernatural parties that treat the relationship as
  real and answerable;
- **return**: a way for absence, damage, exile, or change to be repaired without
  pretending it never occurred.

These are observations, not units to spend. No equation determines legitimacy.
A cruel estate may possess strong anchors; a just refugee camp may have weak
ones. A continuity can survive the loss of one anchor, change under strain, or
be held together badly by force.

Human consequences:

- Institutions become cosmologically relevant because people maintain them,
  not because bureaucracy is secretly magic. A household oven, irrigation
  rotation, military watch, or burial custom can help hold a place together.
- Reform and migration remain possible. A continuity can change honestly when
  it retains recognition and a means of return; preservation is not automatically
  virtuous.
- The Marching Country can attack different anchors through persuasion,
  material seizure, exile, or the demonstration that an institution cannot
  repair what it breaks.

Gameplay consequences:

- Problems offer several comprehensible approaches: repair the seat, resume the
  work, reconcile stakeholders, or reopen return. No practice owns all four.
- Clues can be physical and social. A dry channel, an empty watch, a refused
  homecoming, and a ritual performed without its workers reveal different
  failures.
- Grave Wardens tend toward seat and practice; Ferrymen toward return; Mediums
  and Bonebinders toward recognition and embodied initiative; Renderers toward
  material truth. Those affinities are not exclusive gates.

Limit: do not expose four meters on every scene. The grammar belongs mainly in
authoring and inference; characters use the terms their cultures know.

### 3. The incorporated remain themselves

The Marching Country recruits and incorporates peoples, gods, places, and
institutions rather than replacing everyone with obedient replicas. Successful
incorporation changes which continuities may be challenged and what can reliably
return, but incorporated people retain culture, ambition, kinship, grievance,
and disagreement. Some joined willingly. Some accepted a true liberation and
only later learned its terminal cost. Some serve Nymorai while working to limit
the March. Others use it as cover for an older conquest.

Human consequences:

- The campaign gains direct agents who can bargain, threaten, defect, govern,
  love, and remember. Nymorai need not wake, incarnate, or speak plainly for the
  conflict to have faces.
- A village can welcome one incorporated custom and resist another. Political
  factions do not collapse into possessed and unpossessed camps.
- Liberation movements can have legitimate complaints without making the
  March's endless contest acceptable. Defenders of continuity can be corrupt,
  frightened, humane, or all three.

Gameplay consequences:

- Recurring rivals can change allegiance without a reveal that they were mind
  controlled. Their beliefs, relationships, and evidence determine what they
  will do.
- Victory can divide a coalition, restore a particular return, or deny one
  incorporation without pretending to kill the cosmological power permanently.
- Mundane diplomacy, sabotage, logistics, testimony, and war matter alongside
  occult interventions.

Limit: retain visible consequences of incorporation. If it changes nothing but
uniforms and vocabulary, the March becomes ordinary imperial politics with an
ornate name.

### 4. Divine refuge has many forms; heaven does not have one address

Treat divine refuge and the shelter of the dead as plural achievements rather
than one guaranteed afterlife. A particular god may actually inhabit a mountain,
language, household, procession, species, office, or adopted foreign shrine;
another may travel or act far beyond any house. Dead shelter may be attached to
a god, ancestry, place, route, or practice. No living expert can prove that every
dead person is complete or that all shelters reach the same destination. This
plurality describes divine persons and refuges, not a universal rule that gods
need mortal housing in order to act.

Refugee divinities are therefore possible. A god whose people, species, or land
has been incorporated may arrive with intact customs and dangerous needs. An
empty divine office may continue to organize harvest, law, or weather after the
person who held it is gone, leaving people to endure it, replace it, or let its
work fail.

Human consequences:

- Hospitality to a god is political, material, and religious. A host community
  gains protection or knowledge and accepts demands it may not fully understand.
- Temples can be ports, houses, workshops, courts, or hospitals rather than one
  standardized priestly institution.
- Cultures may revere a Ferryman, refuse a Medium, license a Renderer, or make
  the reverse judgment because their shelters and histories differ.

Gameplay consequences:

- A refugee god can recur as patron, burden, witness, claimant, or factional
  actor without becoming a collectible blessing.
- Players may secure a temporary house, negotiate limits, move a divine object,
  or expose that a supposed god is an empty office still performing its last
  command.
- Necromancy can interact with dead holders and displaced initiative, while
  architecture, hospitality, priesthood, natural magic, and politics provide
  equally credible routes.

Limit: gods should remain exceptional persons, not vending machines for buffs
or practitioners disabled by the absence of the correct site.
Most hospitality scenes concern hungry, injured, frightened, or displaced
people.

### 5. Unfinished countries are bounded scars

Ancient Maldirin expeditions and later Vast-born powers sometimes began to
translate a place into a new order and failed to complete or reverse the work.
The result is an unfinished country: a bounded region where two or more coherent
relationships remain materially true without combining cleanly. It is not a
random dreamscape. Its rivers, distances, bodies, or seasons follow discoverable
but incompatible precedents.

One unfinished country can preserve the strongest implications of the earlier
proposal without making all Maldirin unstable. Such places may exist beyond a
border, under an old city, or inside the remains of a failed imperial work.

Human consequences:

- Settlements adapt through architecture, schedules, marriage custom, animal
  husbandry, and law. Their inhabitants are not merely victims waiting for an
  outsider to explain the anomaly.
- States may exploit unfinished territory as a mine, prison, frontier, refuge,
  or denied history.
- People displaced from one become experts, migrants, claimants, and political
  actors elsewhere.

Gameplay consequences:

- Exploration rewards observation and local knowledge. Rules remain stable
  long enough for planning, then conflict where their precedents meet.
- Route order can determine which local rule the player learns first and which
  guide, tool, or obligation accompanies them into the overlap.
- The region supports corporeal, incorporeal, environmental, political, and
  logistical problems without making every encounter a monster attack.

Limit: use one or two governing contradictions per region. Procedural novelty
must not replace authored consequence or turn navigation into arbitrary riddles.

## Modular candidates

These ideas can coexist with the strong recommendations but should remain
distinct enough to remove without tearing the setting apart.

### The Feast of Forms

A Vast-born appetite or polity regards stable species, bodies, and identities as
an impoverishment of possible form. It offers real restoration, adaptation, and
escape from inherited bodies, then continues past consent and survivable
identity. It works best as a remote campaign power, an incorporated faction, or
a crisis within an unfinished country. Do not reduce it to a mutation bestiary.

### The Good Lady

Retain the prior proposal substantially intact for a remote testament or
singular crisis. Its value lies in the coherence of its own cosmology; stripping
individual motifs into every region would make both it and Dominion weaker.

### Vacant offices

Some divine roles may outlive their holders. The office continues narrow work
without wisdom: a winter boundary closes, a judgment is announced, a species is
called home. This supports institutional horror and succession conflicts, but
it should not imply that every institution has a supernatural executive.

### The returning roads

Some ancient routes were made to return travelers not merely to a location but
to a recognized relationship: citizen, guest, pilgrim, captive, or kin. Broken
roads may deliver a traveler physically while failing that return. This is
strong Ferryman material and a natural pressure point for the March, but it
should wait until threshold play works beyond one plot.

### Vast ecologies

Not every power beyond settled worlds is a sovereign or a god. Species,
weather systems, mineral growths, and predatory relationships can cross into
Maldirin without human motives. They provide nonpolitical danger and wonder,
while local responses still become political.

## Rejected traps

- **Necromancy as the universe's privileged truth.** The player begins as a
  necromancer; the setting does not owe that practice sole access to the answer.
- **The dead as a universal information service.** Testimony remains partial,
  interested, mistaken, coerced, and limited by what a dead person knew.
- **Permanent dream logic.** If every road and household can change rules at any
  moment, neither ordinary labor nor supernatural breach has weight.
- **One secret substance beneath every magic.** Shared terminology must not
  flatten river, fire, distance, divinity, species, and death into colored mana.
- **Ancient sovereignty as rightful order.** Old covenants and captive powers
  can be useful, monstrous, obsolete, or all three.
- **The March as misunderstood neutrality.** Its recruits can be sympathetic;
  its destruction of durable refuge remains a moral fact.
- **Cosmic bookkeeping.** Names, debts, witnesses, and ledgers remain potent
  local instruments, not the source code of reality.
- **Occult chores as world depth.** Cosmological materials and services should
  sharpen decisions, not require routine harvesting, maintenance, or vendor
  circuits before the player may act.
- **Every institution as a cult.** Farms, courts, guilds, households, armies,
  and markets should matter first through the people and work that sustain them.
- **A final boss that solves the premise.** Campaign victories may preserve a
  refuge, break an incorporation, expose an agent, or change a coalition. They
  need not permanently kill the Vast, Nymorai, death, or change itself.

## Proof before canon

Test the recommendations through three campaigns or substantial region frames:

1. **A Dominion frontier:** an incorporated coalition advances through real
   local grievances; the player can defeat a particular incorporation without
   resolving Nymorai.
2. **A non-Dominion civic crisis:** a public work made from the Carrion
   Inheritance fails amid labor, ownership, and survival disputes. Necromancy is
   useful but unnecessary to reach a complete outcome.
3. **A remote cosmological crisis:** the Feast of Forms, the Good Lady, or an
   unfinished country operates under its own coherent pressure rather than
   appearing as another mask of Nymorai.

For each frame, author at least one ordinary household, one labor institution,
one non-necromantic magical specialist, one authority divided by plausible
interests, and one person who benefits from the dangerous order. If the
cosmology cannot be shown through their work, shelter, travel, injury, and
choices, it needs less vocabulary and more consequence.
