# Practice and tradition ideation pass

## Status

This is a research-backed design pass, not settled canon. It supersedes the
roster recommendation in [class-roster-reconsideration.md](class-roster-reconsideration.md)
without erasing that memo's useful criticism. It was written before the magic
contract stopped treating environmental situatedness as a system-wide design
identity. The corrected review below preserves concepts whose repeated object
survives that change and reopens those that depended upon it. In particular:

- **Medium, Bonebinder, and Renderer** remain the strongest provisional core.
- **Summoner** returns as a serious starting-practice candidate rather than a
  discarded predecessor of Bonebinder.
- A **mortality practice** is now a serious additional-seat candidate. Its
  repeated object is the practitioner's own vital state, and its direct loop is
  suspend, commit, fight, and return.
- **Anatomist** is more coherent as a major Bonebinder tradition than as a
  separate practice.
- **Grave Warden/Cairnwright** retains useful field-control mechanics but no
  longer earns a seat merely by establishing local conditions; its necromantic
  basis remains unresolved.
- **Ferryman** remains regional or advanced until Maldirin earns its implied
  account of passage after death.

The working vocabulary is useful but also provisional:

- A **practice** owns a repeatable game object and decision loop.
- A **tradition** is a learned lineage, doctrine, or method that changes how a
  practice uses that object and what its practitioners believe they are doing.

Maldirin's societies need not agree with this taxonomy. It is a design tool
first and a possible player-facing classification second.

## Research method and cautions

The sources below are stimuli, not packages to import into Maldirin.
Historically or religiously situated practices must not be stripped of their
context and pasted into a fantasy taxonomy. Their useful contribution is often
a question or contrast:

- A British Museum shabti entry preserves two coexisting interpretations: an
  image of the deceased and a substitute laborer. That is a valuable design
  tension between **recognizable dead persons** and **constructed service**, not
  permission to reproduce Egyptian funerary religion.
- Medieval reliquaries show how a fragment of a body can remain attached to a
  particular person, history, institution, and shaped container. That supports
  provenance-bound play, but not a universal rule that all remains work like
  Christian relics.
- *Frankenstein* keeps creation tied to the creator's responsibility toward an
  intelligent made being. It cautions against treating constructed bodies as
  equipment with no agency merely because their parts can be changed.
- Action RPGs demonstrate the immediate pleasure of commanding undead, but
  also a recurring scope failure: one necromancer archetype absorbs minions,
  corpses, curses, blood, bone, decay, self-sacrifice, and control magic until
  its identity is just “all death-colored mechanics.”

The design should use the contrasts while developing its own institutions,
materials, metaphysics, and moral disagreements.

## Roster recommendation

| Seat | Repeated game object | Strongest current disposition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Medium | the player's body shared with dead initiative | retain and deepen |
| Bonebinder | one durable corporeal dead body | retain; split preservation from composition |
| Renderer | limited preparations made from death-altered matter | retain; split refinement from cultivation |
| Summoner | a bounded formation of external dead actors | promote to starting-practice proof |
| Mortality practice (working concept) | the practitioner's own vital state | promote to focused fifth-seat proof |
| Cairnwright / rebuilt Grave Warden | prepared mortuary fittings and bounded ground | reopen its basis before another proof |
| Ferryman | a locally established mode of passage | regional tradition or advanced practice |

This is deliberately **four strong concepts, one new fifth-seat candidate, and
open alternatives**, not a final five. Medium, Bonebinder, Renderer, and
Summoner have credible distinct loops. The mortality practice has a credible
object and martial loop but has not yet earned a roster seat through play.
Cairnwright must first explain what necromantic capacity travels in its prepared
fittings and why its lines affect the bodies or dead it claims to govern. Only
then should play prove whether that spatial control is more fun than distributing
its best techniques among equipment, scenes, and other practices.

## Medium

### Shared practice

The Medium makes the living self temporarily available to dead initiative.
The player repeatedly chooses whom or what to admit, how much bodily control to
yield, when to draw on the guest's capacities, and when to force or negotiate a
separation.

The Medium does **not** own all testimony, names, promises, or consent magic.
A guest may lie, misunderstand, refuse, or know less than the living expected.

### Tradition A: Guest House

Guest House Mediums preserve a recognizable dead personality and negotiate a
bounded residence. They favor a few known guests whose habits, relationships,
and disagreements accumulate across a testament.

Direct play:

- invite one guest into a stance before or during danger;
- yield a particular faculty, limb, voice, or reflex rather than the whole
  body;
- let the guest take an autonomous reaction at the risk of conflicting intent;
- strengthen a guest by honoring its nature rather than feeding a generic
  meter; and
- dismiss, reconcile with, or lose access to a guest as relationships change.

Guest House criticism of its rival: reducing a dead person to useful fragments
is a second death committed for convenience.

### Tradition B: Borrowed Faculty

Borrowed Faculty Mediums avoid sustained habitation. They isolate a dead
person's reflex, perception, voice, or practiced motion and bear that fragment
for a short, intense purpose.

Direct play:

- prepare a small set of faculties from known dead;
- combine incompatible capacities at mounting bodily cost;
- switch quickly without granting a guest durable initiative; and
- deliberately burn away a faculty for one exceptional act.

Borrowed Faculty criticism of Guest House: carrying another will inside the
living body is not respect merely because the invitation was polite.

### Proof burden

Both traditions must be more than stance loadouts. Guest House requires
legible recurring persons; Borrowed Faculty requires mechanically specific
fragments and visible consequences for what is discarded.

## Bonebinder

### Shared practice

The Bonebinder maintains one durable corporeal servant. Position, anatomy,
equipment, repair, and the initiative seated in the body persist beyond one
fight. The practice owns a **second body**, not a generic minion count.

### Tradition A: Conservator

The Conservator treats the body's inherited form as evidence and capability.
Work restores, braces, or carefully improves what belonged to that particular
body. Its history matters: a mason's shoulder, a courier's feet, an old
fracture, and a missing hand are not interchangeable statistics.

Direct play:

- repair or brace specific parts without erasing their history;
- draw out remembered posture and labor;
- accept limitation when replacement would falsify the body; and
- achieve “uplift” through exact restoration, articulation, and respectful
  augmentation rather than idealized reconstruction.

### Tradition B: Compositor

The Compositor treats anatomy as a planned structure. It replaces, joins,
distorts, and adapts remains for an anticipated purpose: a broad interposing
frame, a low body for tunnels, additional grasping structures, or a frame made
to carry a particular tool.

Direct play:

- commit to a small number of silhouette-changing modules;
- trade equipment compatibility, mobility, reach, defense, and treatment
  requirements rather than adding flat coefficients;
- change the servant only through material work at an appropriate site; and
- confront what degree of initiative belongs to a body whose original form has
  been overwritten.

The Conservator regards composition as mutilation disguised as craft. The
Compositor regards preservation as sentimentality that forces a dead body to
keep every failure of its first life. Neither position is automatically right,
and individual practitioners may be hypocritical or moderate.

### Why Anatomist is not presently separate

The macabre-golem fantasy, visible modular anatomy, and creator/created tension
all strengthen Bonebinder's persistent second-body loop. Moving them into a
separate Anatomist practice would leave the Bonebinder artificially narrow and
force two practices to compete for the same remains, repair UI, visual rig, and
anatomical decision space.

Self-grafting can still exist as rare cross-practice advancement, a regional
school, or a later expert path. It should not define a starter seat until it
proves a repeatable loop that is not “Bonebinder, but the second body is me.”

## Renderer

### Shared practice

The Renderer identifies and prepares properties in matter altered by death.
The repeated loop is gather selectively, diagnose, prepare a limited kit, and
commit applications whose interactions are grounded in their materials.

The Renderer does not automatically become the best medic, alchemist,
craftsperson, or forensic investigator. Mundane skill still matters.

### Tradition A: Refiner

Refiners isolate a property, arrest unwanted change, and make narrow
preparations reliable. They value exact heat, separation, storage, measured
doses, and known reactions.

Direct play favors coatings, charges, antidotes, counters, and combinations
prepared before danger. Its advantage is confidence; its cost is inflexibility
and the loss of qualities excluded during refinement.

### Tradition B: Cultivator

Cultivators keep a transformation alive. They seed carrion beds, maintain
ferments, carry active cultures, and exploit decay or succession as it changes
through place and time.

Direct play favors adaptive hazards, growth across a field, reactions to local
material, and preparations that become stronger or stranger if maintained.
Its advantage is responsiveness; its costs are containment, time, and the risk
that a living process ceases to be obedient.

Refiners call cultivation contamination with a philosophy attached.
Cultivators call refinement the destruction of every relationship that made a
material powerful.

### Proof burden

The two traditions must change the loop, not merely offer stable versus random
damage. A Cultivator should alter a locality and revisit consequences; a
Refiner should make commitments during preparation that cannot be solved by
opening inventory mid-fight.

## Summoner

### Shared practice

The Summoner calls and coordinates a **bounded formation of external dead
actors**. Where the Bonebinder invests in one corporeal body, the Summoner
manages composition, cohesion, duration, and collective intent. Where the
Medium admits dead initiative into the self, the Summoner remains bodily
separate and must keep several wills or impressions acting together.

The Summoner should recover the immediate pleasure of raising and commanding
the dead without turning the game into either an idle army simulator or
per-unit clerical work.

### Tradition A: Retinue

The Retinue keeps a few recognizable shades. Each has a name, a strong
disposition, and one field role. They may disagree, remember dismissals, form
relationships with one another, and become more capable through familiarity.

Direct play:

- field perhaps two to four meaningful shades, not a crowd;
- issue formation intent—hold, converge, screen, pursue, withdraw—then allow
  characteristic individual execution;
- spend attention to override one shade at a critical moment;
- combine relationships as well as combat roles; and
- risk departure, refusal, or divided action without making every shade a
  dialogue-management chore.

### Tradition B: Levy

The Levy carries prepared calls or remains sufficient to form a modest temporary
collective. A death-marked site or sufficiently violent recent history can alter,
enlarge, or make stranger what answers. Its members are not treated as returned
complete persons. They are overlapping impressions, substitute labor, and
repeated motions given brief common direction.

Direct play:

- shape the host into a few semantic units rather than dozens of actors;
- consolidate many weak impressions into a wall, rush, drag line, carrying
  party, or grasping field;
- split a unit to cover more ground at reduced cohesion;
- let the formation spend itself accomplishing physical work; and
- decide whether a distinct person emerging from the collective must be
  released, recruited, or suppressed.

The Retinue regards the Levy as a convenient doctrine for ignoring persons
inside a mass. The Levy regards the Retinue as aristocratic necromancy that
grants individuality only to useful or charismatic dead.

### Gameplay rules learned from other games

Action RPG precedents support customization and commanding an undead host, but
they also expose constraints Maldirin should handle differently:

- Baseline usefulness cannot depend on conveniently finding fresh corpses.
  Prepared anchors or a small starting formation guarantee a floor; local dead
  change composition or offer exceptional opportunities.
- The player chooses an **order first and a target or area second**. Units act
  in a readable sequence rather than all snapping at once.
- Autonomous standing orders prevent command tax, while limited overrides
  preserve player agency.
- The field should expose health, remaining manifestation, cohesion, and next
  intent for each semantic unit.
- A practical cap of a few meaningful units protects legibility, performance,
  attachment, and animation quality. “More bodies” can be represented inside a
  unit's visual rig and statistics rather than as another full actor.
- Dismissal or sacrifice may be viable, but it must carry tactical,
  relational, or material cost rather than becoming free resource conversion.

### Compatibility warning

The current executable uses the legacy ID `summoner` for what is now presented
as Bonebinder and stores Bonebinder state separately. A restored Summoner must
not simply reclaim that ID. Before implementation, write an explicit migration
that gives Bonebinder a stable public identity, preserves old testaments, and
introduces a distinct Summoner practice state and content pack.

## Martial formation, mortality practice, and Revenancy

`Martial necromancer` does not name one thing. Arms, armor, fieldcraft, and
anti-manifestation training can belong to a regional formation whose members
come from several practices. A Medium, Bonebinder, Renderer, or Summoner should
be able to become a convincing warrior-necromancer without changing practice.
This preserves martial character breadth instead of granting one class every
weapon-centered fantasy.

The separate fifth-seat candidate manipulates the practitioner's **own vital
state**. Its loop is to suspend breath, circulation, sensation, pain, warmth, or
another living function; commit the resulting state through movement, defense,
perception, or weapon work; endure consequences that may be deferred or harder
to read; and recall the function before the return itself becomes dangerous.
The working traditions are opposed:

- **The Last Vigil** treats safe return and preserved mortal integrity as the
  discipline's proof.
- **The Pale Discipline** accepts lasting suspension and bodily alteration in
  exchange for deeper commitment.

Neither doctrine is an alignment. The practice earns a starting seat only if
this loop changes positioning, weapons, wounds, treatment, retreat, and
noncombat traversal rather than becoming a generic melee class with pale spell
effects.

**Revenancy is not this practice and is not routine character creation.** It is
a rare campaign transformation in which a character retains their own
initiative in their dead body through a discovered, practice-specific method,
controlled death, irreversible cost, and actual risk of failure. It preserves
and alters the original practice. Lichdom is the greater and separate feat of
making initiative durable beyond one body's destruction.

The complete working contract, practice-specific routes, costs, and rejection
tests live in
[Martial necromancy and Revenancy](martial-necromancy-and-revenancy.md).

## Cairnwright / rebuilt Grave Warden

### Shared candidate

This candidate uses grave stakes, cord, stones, nails, ash, prepared fittings,
and appropriate remains to make bounded ground. The player establishes a small
number of visible lines that enemies can cross, break, seize, bypass, or turn.
The equipment and geometry are legible; the source of their necromantic force is
not yet settled. A valid revision must name what has been prepared or altered,
what kinds of body or initiative it can affect, and the cost of making a line
operative without appealing to generic continuity or environmental permission.

### Tradition A: Sanctuary

Sanctuary builds refuge, exclusion, stabilization, and defensible retreat. It
protects particular bodies or a bounded place and can deliberately collapse a
refuge to save what remains within it.

### Tradition B: Siegecraft

Siegecraft redirects movement, exposes intruders, closes escape, and consumes
anchors for violent changes in the field. It accepts that a boundary is a tool
of coercion as readily as protection.

### Outstanding weakness

This is tactically promising but ontologically less secure than the other four.
If prepared anchors affect every living enemy, it risks becoming generic trap
magic with mortuary nouns. If it only works in graves or under legal custody, it
is an office or regional technique rather than a reliable starting practice.
Death-altered places may change or amplify its operation, but that cannot stand
in for defining the portable act.

“Grave Warden” may ultimately be a Sanctuary lineage or public office rather
than the practice's universal name.

## Ferryman and other demotions

### Ferryman

Keep the title and its best ideas—escort, retrieval, merciful endings, denial
of recurrence, and dangerous crossings—but attach them to cultures and places
that actually possess an established mode of dead passage. It can become:

- a regional tradition available to more than one practice;
- an advanced practice learned after encountering a particular passage; or
- a later starting practice if several materially different regions prove a
  shared loop without imposing one universal afterlife.

### Reliquarian

Provenance-bound remains, shaped containers, institutional ownership, theft,
and contested identity are excellent item and faction material. They presently
overlap Medium guests, Renderer preparations, and Summoner anchors too heavily
to justify a starting practice. Build them as a cross-practice equipment and
relationship system first.

### Self-grafter and Revenant transformations

These are distinct late commitments. A self-grafter materially reconstructs a
living or otherwise continuing body and may arise from Bonebinder, Renderer, a
regional school, or another learned method. A Revenant retains their own
initiative in their own dead body through a dangerous, discovered route. Either
can change equipment, anatomy, healing, social recognition, continuity, and
visual rigs extensively; neither should be mistaken for the mortality practice
or offered at ordinary character creation merely to supply its transformation
fantasy.

### Curser or Doomsayer

Curses belong in Maldirin, but “the necromancer who imposes bad conditions” is
not yet a practice. It readily collapses into generic debuffs or the recurring
anti-pattern in which names, debts, promises, and reciprocity become universal
occult laws. Curses should arise from specific guests, remains, preparations,
Gods, items, and local practices until a distinct repeated object emerges.

## Cross-practice overlap test

| Situation | Medium | Bonebinder | Renderer | Summoner | Cairnwright |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| ordinary armed fight | change the player's embodied capability | coordinate one durable second body | commit prepared applications | direct a bounded formation | reshape bounded ground |
| rescue under pressure | lend a dead faculty to the living effort | put a maintained body where living risk is greatest | counter smoke, rot, poison, fire, or material failure | distribute labor and screening | establish a temporary safe line |
| incorporeal threat | host or contest initiative | use a prepared body as anchor/interceptor | expose or alter its material relation | meet it with external dead actors | constrain where it may manifest or cross |
| aftermath | negotiate separation and memory | repair anatomy and settle custody | render selectively and leave evidence | decide what the formation retains or releases | recover, abandon, or record anchors |

If the same feature can be moved between columns by changing its flavor noun,
the feature is not specific enough. Not every practice needs a bespoke answer
to every scene; mundane skills, equipment, companions, retreat, and refusal
remain real play.

The mortality practice is intentionally withheld from this table until a
stripped prototype proves that changing one's vital state produces distinct,
fair decisions in all four situations. A fifth column should be earned by play,
not by completing the roster on paper.

## Recommended proof sequence

1. **Summoner command proof.** Add a noncanonical direct-play character with
   three semantic units. Test order-then-target control, sequential initiative,
   autonomous standing orders, cohesion, duration, health disclosure, and one
   noncombat labor use. Reject the design if the player mostly watches or
   performs per-unit chores.
2. **Bonebinder doctrine proof.** Give the same persistent servant either three
   provenance-preserving repairs or three silhouette-changing composite
   modules. Each path must alter navigation, combat, treatment, equipment, and
   how at least one NPC responds.
3. **Medium doctrine paper proof.** Resolve the same three scenes using a
   recurring named guest and using short-lived faculties. The outcomes should
   differ in relationship, bodily risk, and available action—not only prose.
4. **Renderer persistence proof.** Let a Refiner prepare a fixed kit and a
   Cultivator establish an active process before the same route. Revisit the
   locality later to prove that cultivation is world interaction rather than a
   damage-over-time skin.
5. **Mortality-practice proof.** In one stripped martial encounter, make
   suspending and recalling functions change at least three decisions beyond
   damage coefficients. Carry the resulting state through wounds and treatment,
   and verify that deferred consequences are legible rather than surprise
   punishment.
6. **Revenancy paper proof.** Model one failed and one successful transformation
   through testament continuity. Failure must remain genuine character death;
   success must preserve the original practice while imposing irreversible
   bodily, social, and successor consequences.
7. **Cairnwright basis proof.** Before another combat prototype, write one
   portable act in source/method/consequence form and identify what targets it
   cannot affect. Then play one ordinary road fight and one death-altered site
   with the prose stripped. Reject or demote the seat if its fittings are generic
   traps, if the second site merely supplies a larger coefficient, or if the
   first scene has no credible necromantic operation.
8. **Roster comparison.** Run all surviving practices through an armed fight,
   rescue, supernatural threat, social entrance, retreat, loot decision, and
   safe recovery. Measure distinct decisions, not damage parity alone.

## Sources used as design stimuli

- [Diablo IV Quarterly Update—June 2022](https://news.blizzard.com/en-us/article/23816542/diablo-iv-quarterly-updatejune-2022): corpses as a controlled resource, customizable minion roles, and an explicit army-versus-independent choice.
- [Diablo II manual](https://download.blizzard.com/pub/misc/Diablo%20II%20Manual.pdf): a small host partly animated by retained will, and a constructed golem that inherits properties from its material.
- [Last Epoch Necromancer update](https://forum.lastepoch.com/t/necromancer-updates-coming-in-patch-0-8-3/42468/1): active versus relaxed summoner play and the balance/performance problems caused by unbounded unit counts.
- [Grim Dawn Necromancer guide](https://www.grimdawn.com/guide/character/masteries/necromancer/): a useful warning about allowing one archetype to own minions, vitality, decay, martial play, and other death-associated mechanics at once.
- [British Museum shabti of Sunero](https://www.britishmuseum.org/collection/object/Y_EA65206): coexistence of deceased representation and substitute labor.
- [The Metropolitan Museum of Art, “Relics and Reliquaries in Medieval Christianity”](https://www.metmuseum.org/essays/relics-and-reliquaries-in-medieval-christianity): person-specific remains, institutional custody, costly containers, and reliquaries shaped like body parts.
- [Mary Shelley, *Frankenstein; or, The Modern Prometheus*](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/41445/41445-h/41445-h.htm): the made being's agency and the creator's responsibility toward it.
- [Harvard Theological Review, “Oil Lamps, Spearheads, and Skulls”](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/harvard-theological-review/article/oil-lamps-spearheads-and-skulls-possible-evidence-of-necromancy-during-late-antiquity-in-the-teomim-cave-judean-hills/973DF6E86AF609B8B4D1296D8292B8EB): a reminder that alleged necromantic practice was material, situated, and interpretively uncertain rather than a clean universal system.
